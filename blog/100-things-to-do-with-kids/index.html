100 Things to Do With Kids Before They Grow Up

September 2026 · 12 min read

Childhood goes fast. One day they want to hold your hand everywhere you go, and the next they're asking for the car keys. The years in between are full of ordinary days that blur together unless you fill them with something worth remembering.

This is a family bucket list. Not a Pinterest-perfect checklist. Just 100 real things you can do with your kids, organized by category, that range from free-and-easy to all-day adventures. You won't do them all. You don't need to. Pick the ones that sound like your family and start there.

Outdoor Adventures (1-20)

  1. Camp in the backyard (or the living room, if that's more your speed)
  2. Go on a sunrise hike and eat breakfast at the top
  3. Build a fort out of sticks and leaves in the woods
  4. Catch fireflies in a jar and release them before bed
  5. Swim in a lake, river, or ocean — not just a pool
  6. Have a water balloon fight with no rules
  7. Plant a tree together and measure it every year
  8. Go berry picking and make something with what you find
  9. Ride bikes somewhere you've never been
  10. Fly a kite on the windiest day you can find
  11. Skip rocks on a pond and count the bounces
  12. Stargaze away from city lights and find three constellations
  13. Build a sandcastle with an actual moat
  14. Go fishing, even if you catch nothing
  15. Walk barefoot in the mud after a rainstorm
  16. Find a waterfall and stand near it
  17. Climb a tree (a real one, not a playground structure)
  18. Have a picnic somewhere beautiful
  19. Explore a cave or rock formation
  20. Watch a thunderstorm from a safe, covered spot

Rainy Day Activities (21-35)

  1. Build the most elaborate blanket fort you can manage
  2. Have a movie marathon with homemade popcorn
  3. Write and perform a family play
  4. Do a 1,000-piece puzzle together over a weekend
  5. Make homemade play dough from scratch
  6. Have an indoor treasure hunt with hand-drawn maps
  7. Build something out of cardboard boxes — a spaceship, a castle, a car
  8. Record a family podcast or radio show
  9. Play board games until someone flips the board (then play again)
  10. Learn a card game your grandparents played
  11. Make friendship bracelets
  12. Write letters to your future selves and hide them
  13. Create a stop-motion animation with toys
  14. Build a marble run out of household materials
  15. Read an entire book out loud together in one sitting

In the Kitchen (36-50)

  1. Bake a birthday cake from scratch — no box mix
  2. Make pasta by hand
  3. Cook a meal from a country you've never visited
  4. Have a pizza night where everyone makes their own
  5. Make ice cream without a machine (bag method works)
  6. Grow herbs on the windowsill and cook with them
  7. Decorate cookies with absurd amounts of frosting
  8. Have a "chopped" competition with mystery ingredients from the fridge
  9. Make soup on the coldest day of the year
  10. Try a fruit or vegetable none of you have ever eaten
  11. Make jam or preserves from fresh fruit
  12. Bake bread and wait for it to rise (patience lesson included)
  13. Create a family recipe book with everyone's favorites
  14. Have breakfast for dinner
  15. Make hot chocolate from real chocolate

Creative Projects (51-65)

  1. Paint a mural on a large piece of paper (or a wall, if you're brave)
  2. Make a time capsule and bury it in the yard
  3. Create a family newspaper or magazine
  4. Make a short film with a plot, costumes, and credits
  5. Build something with wood — a birdhouse, a shelf, a stool
  6. Tie-dye t-shirts in the backyard
  7. Learn to knit or crochet something simple together
  8. Press flowers and frame them
  9. Make candles or soap
  10. Write a song and record it
  11. Create a comic book with original characters
  12. Do a family self-portrait in any medium
  13. Make a scrapbook of a family trip
  14. Design and sew a simple stuffed animal
  15. Collaborate on a story — each person writes one chapter

Learning and Discovery (66-80)

  1. Visit a museum and let the kids lead the tour
  2. Learn five words in a new language together
  3. Watch a documentary and discuss it over dinner
  4. Go to a planetarium or observatory
  5. Volunteer together at a food bank or shelter
  6. Visit a farm and learn where food actually comes from
  7. Take apart an old appliance to see how it works
  8. Learn to read a paper map and navigate somewhere
  9. Attend a live performance — theater, concert, ballet, anything
  10. Start a family book club
  11. Do a science experiment at the kitchen table
  12. Visit a historical site in your own town
  13. Learn basic first aid together
  14. Identify 10 birds, trees, or wildflowers in your neighborhood
  15. Write a letter to someone you admire and actually mail it

Seasonal and Holiday Traditions (81-100)

  1. Pick pumpkins and carve them with ridiculous faces
  2. Go apple picking and make apple crisp that afternoon
  3. Drive around to see holiday lights with hot chocolate in the car
  4. Make valentines by hand for everyone in the family
  5. Jump in the biggest leaf pile you can find
  6. Build a snowman (or a sand snowman, if snow isn't your climate)
  7. Have a New Year's Eve countdown — even if bedtime is 9pm
  8. Plant a garden in the spring and eat from it in the summer
  9. Go to a farmers market and let each kid pick one thing
  10. Have a back-to-school family dinner tradition
  11. Make ornaments for the tree each year
  12. Do a spring cleaning together where everyone donates something
  13. Watch the sunrise on the first day of summer
  14. Go on a fall color drive with no destination
  15. Create a family holiday card with an outtake photo
  16. Have a mid-year "family meeting" where everyone shares a highlight
  17. Run through sprinklers on the hottest day of the year
  18. Build a gingerbread house (structural failures are part of it)
  19. Take a family photo in the same spot every year
  20. End each year by looking through photos together and picking your favorites

The list isn't the point

You don't need to do all 100. Some will never fit your family. Others you'll do so often they stop feeling special (until years later, when they become the memories your kids talk about most).

The point is to be intentional. Childhood doesn't wait for you to be less busy, less tired, or more prepared. It just keeps moving.

Document the ones you do. A photo book of your family's adventure list is the kind of thing they'll want when they're 30.
