AI Photo Book From iPhone: Turn Your Camera Roll Into a Book

You can turn your iPhone camera roll into a photo book without selecting or organizing photos. Share an iCloud link, AI picks your best shots and removes duplicates, then a designer lays out every page. First proof in 72 hours.

November 2026 · 9 min read
Written by Olga Miller, founder of ReMeet

Your iPhone camera roll has thousands of photos. Birthday candles, first steps, beach sunsets, random screenshots, 14 nearly identical shots of the same toddler smile. Somewhere in there is a beautiful photo book. The problem is getting it out.

With AI photo book services, you don't have to sort, pick, or arrange anything yourself. You share a link to your photos, AI does the heavy lifting, and a designer builds the book. Here's exactly how it works with an iPhone.

Why iPhone photos are perfect for a photo book

iPhones have been taking excellent photos for years. If you have an iPhone 12 or newer, your photos are sharp enough for full-page prints in a hardcover book. Even older models produce photos that look great at standard photo book sizes.

But the real advantage is quantity. Because your phone is always with you, it captures the moments that matter: the messy kitchen on a birthday morning, the dog asleep on the couch, the look on your kid's face when they see snow for the first time. These are the photos that make the best books.

The downside? You have too many of them. The average iPhone user takes over 2,000 photos a year. Scrolling through all of them to pick the best 80 for a book is a job most people start and never finish.

The problem: thousands of photos, no time to sort

You've probably thought about making a photo book before. Maybe you even started one on Shutterfly or Mixbook. You uploaded 200 photos, spent 45 minutes dragging them into a template, realized you were only on page 6, and closed the tab.

That's not a personal failing. It's a design problem. Traditional photo book editors ask you to do the work of a photo curator and a graphic designer at the same time. Picking the best 80 photos out of 2,000 is hard. Arranging them into a layout that looks good is harder. Doing both on a weeknight after the kids are in bed is nearly impossible.

AI changes this equation. Instead of you sorting through every photo, the AI scores them all for quality, sharpness, faces, composition, and variety. It removes the duplicates (all 14 of that toddler smile), drops the blurry ones, and picks the strongest set. Then a human designer builds the book from that curated selection.

How to share photos from your iPhone

The fastest way to send your photos to ReMeet is with an iCloud link. Here's how:

Step 1: Open the Photos app

Go to the album or time period you want in your book. This could be a specific album ("Summer 2026"), all photos from a date range, or your entire library.

Step 2: Select the photos

Tap "Select" in the upper right corner. You can tap individual photos, or swipe across rows to select many at once. For a whole album, tap "Select All." Don't worry about picking only the good ones. Send everything. The AI will sort it out.

Step 3: Tap Share, then Copy iCloud Link

Tap the share button (the square with the arrow) at the bottom left. Scroll down and tap "Copy iCloud Link." Your iPhone will prepare the link. This can take a few seconds if you selected hundreds of photos.

Step 4: Paste the link in the ReMeet order form

Go to remeet.cc, pick your book type and page count, and paste the iCloud link in the photo link field. That's it. You're done.

Important: don't use a Shared Album

Apple compresses photos in Shared Albums to save storage. The compressed versions look fine on a phone screen but print soft and slightly blurry. Always use the "Copy iCloud Link" method from your regular Photos library or a personal album. This sends the full-resolution originals.

What happens after you share the link

Once you paste the link and place your order, the process runs without you. Here's what happens behind the scenes:

1. AI downloads and analyzes every photo. The system pulls all your photos from the iCloud link and scores each one. It checks sharpness, lighting, faces, composition, and how similar each photo is to others in the set. Near-duplicates get grouped, and the strongest version is kept. Here's how the AI selection works in detail.

2. Photos are sorted by date and grouped by event. The AI reads the timestamp and location data in each photo's metadata. Photos from the same day or event are grouped together. A birthday party, a vacation week, and a regular Tuesday at home each get their own section.

3. A designer builds the layout. A real person takes the AI's curated selection and designs every page. Full-bleed photos, multi-image spreads, white space, captions. The layout varies page by page. No template grids, no repeated patterns.

4. You receive a proof. Within 72 hours, you get a designed PDF to review. Flip through it, tell us what to change in plain words ("swap the photo on page 8," "add more beach photos," "move the birthday section earlier"). Two rounds of revisions are included.

What about Live Photos and videos?

Your iPhone camera roll probably has a mix of regular photos, Live Photos, and short videos. Here's how each type works:

Regular photos: Included as-is. This is the core of your book.

Live Photos: The still frame is included automatically. Live Photos are essentially a photo with a short video clip attached. The photo part goes into your book at full resolution.

Videos: Not included by default, but you can add the video frame extraction add-on for $50. The AI pulls the best still frames from your videos. This is especially useful for moments you caught on video but forgot to photograph: a first word, a dance recital, blowing out birthday candles. Those frames go into the book alongside your photos.

What if you have photos on multiple devices?

Many families have photos split across phones, tablets, cameras, and cloud accounts. This is fine. You can share multiple links in one order:

  • An iCloud link from your iPhone
  • A Google Photos album link from your partner's Android phone
  • A Dropbox or Google Drive folder from your DSLR photos

The AI combines everything into one pool, sorts by date, removes duplicates across all sources, and builds a single cohesive book. No need to merge albums yourself first.

iPhone vs. other photo sources

ReMeet works with any photo source, but iPhone photos have a few specific advantages for photo books:

  • Rich metadata. iPhone photos include precise timestamps, GPS location, and even scene type (portrait, landscape, macro). The AI uses all of this to sort and group your photos intelligently.
  • Consistent quality. Because one camera took all the photos, the colors and tones are consistent across the book. Mixed sources (phone + DSLR + tablet) sometimes produce visible shifts in color and grain.
  • iCloud sharing is fast. An iCloud link gives the AI direct access to full-resolution files. No uploading, no waiting for progress bars, no file size limits.
  • Portrait mode photos print beautifully. The depth effect that blurs the background on portrait-mode shots translates well to print, giving your book a professional feel without a professional camera.

Common concerns

Will the AI pick the right photos?

The AI scores every photo for technical quality (sharpness, lighting, exposure) and content (faces, groups, variety). It's good at eliminating obvious bad photos: blurry shots, screenshots, near-duplicates. It's also good at identifying strong candidates: clear faces, good light, interesting composition. But it doesn't know which moments matter most to you. That's why you review the proof and can request changes. If a specific photo is missing, just say so.

Are my photos private?

Yes. Your photos are encrypted in transit and at rest. They are used only to build your book. We delete them 90 days after your book is complete. Full details are in the privacy policy.

What if I don't like the result?

You review a designed PDF proof before anything is printed. Tell us what to change in plain words: "add the photo of us at the lighthouse," "swap pages 4 and 7," "remove the blurry one on page 12." Two rounds of revisions are included with every book.

What if I have photos on both iPhone and Android?

Send both links. You can share an iCloud link for your iPhone photos and a Google Photos link for your Android photos. The AI combines everything into one book, sorted by date. Duplicates across both sources are automatically removed.

No. The AI only sorts, scores, and selects your photos. It never applies filters, removes objects, or generates anything. Every photo in your book is a real, unedited photo from your camera roll. A human designer checks every page.

What about my Live Photos and videos?
