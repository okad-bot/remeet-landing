Do You Use AI? How a Designer and Software Make One Book

Updated Nov 2026
Olga, designer at ReMeet, 10 years in editorial design

I use software only to sort: find duplicates, blurry shots, and the best frame of a burst. I never change or generate your photos. Every page is designed and checked by a person. Your designed book from $29, first proof in 72 hours.

People ask me this all the time. "Is it AI?" "Does a computer design my book?" Fair question. The short version: software does the tedious part, I do the creative part. Here is exactly how that splits.

What the software does vs what the designer does

Two jobs, clearly separated. The software never touches layout or design. The designer never sorts 2,000 photos by hand.

Why auto-generated books all look the same

Services like Chatbooks and Google Photos auto-books work differently. There is no designer. Software picks photos, drops them into templates, and outputs a finished book in minutes. Nobody looks at it before it prints.

The result is predictable. Every book follows the same template rotation: one photo, two photos, three-photo grid, repeat. The captions are generic ("Summer 2026"). The crops are automatic, which means group shots lose the person on the edge, landscapes lose the horizon, and tall photos get squeezed into square frames.

Open two Chatbooks side by side, one from a wedding and one from a camping trip. The layouts are identical. The template does not know what is in the photos. It fills slots.

That works for a quick monthly dump. It does not work when you care about how the book feels. A gift for grandparents, a baby's first year, a wedding album. These need a person who understands that the ceremony spread should be quiet and the dance floor spread should be crowded.

What gets checked before printing

Before you see your proof, every page goes through a checklist. This is the part AI cannot do.

  • Every crop is manual. I check that faces are complete, hands are not cut at the wrist, and horizons are straight. Auto-crop gets this wrong more often than people realize.
  • Photo pairs make sense. Two photos on a spread should relate. A morning coffee shot next to an evening sunset, not a parking lot next to a birthday cake.
  • The book has rhythm. Full-bleed pages alternate with white-space pages. Busy spreads follow calm ones. The pacing keeps you turning pages instead of zoning out.
  • No duplicates or near-duplicates. The software catches most of these, but I check again. Two shots from the same angle five seconds apart, the software flags them as different. A person sees they are the same moment.
  • Text is readable. If you sent captions, notes, or dates, they sit where the eye finds them without covering the photo.
  • The story flows. A travel book starts with departure, moves through the trip, and ends with the return. A baby book goes month by month. The order is not just chronological, it is intentional.

After you review the proof, you get two rounds of changes. Tell me in plain words: "swap the photo on page 7," "move the beach shot earlier," "add more space around the family portrait." No design jargon needed.

How it works in practice

  1. You share a link. iCloud album, Google Photos folder, Dropbox, any cloud link. No uploading individual photos. How to share a Google Photos link.
  2. Software sorts your photos. Duplicates removed, blurry shots filtered, images grouped by date. This takes seconds, even for 3,000 photos.
  3. I design the book. Every page, by hand. I pick which photos stay, choose layouts for each spread, crop and position each image, and add white space where the book needs to breathe.
  4. You review and approve. See the proof, request changes in plain words, approve when you are happy. Then download the PDF or order a printed copy.

The whole process takes about 72 hours from the moment you share your photos. More about the design service.

Frequently asked questions

Does ReMeet use AI to generate images?

No. I never generate, edit, or alter your photos. The software only sorts: it finds duplicates, removes blurry shots, picks the sharpest frame from a burst, and groups images by date. Every page is then designed by a person.

Can I see the AI selections before the designer starts?

I review the software's selections and adjust them before building the book. You see the result as a designed proof within 72 hours. If any photo should be swapped, tell me in plain words during the revision round.

What software does ReMeet use?

Proprietary sorting software that scores photos for sharpness, exposure, and composition, detects duplicates, and groups images by date and location. I work in professional layout tools to build every page by hand.

Is an AI-only photo book good enough for a gift?

For a casual gift, it can work. For grandparents, a wedding, or a milestone, the template layouts and auto-cropping usually show. A designer-checked book has better flow, careful cropping, and pages that feel intentional.

How is ReMeet different from Chatbooks or Google Photos auto-books?

Chatbooks and Google Photos auto-books are fully automated: software picks photos and fills templates with no human review. I use software only for sorting, then design every page by hand, check every crop, and structure the book as a story.
