Anniversary Photo Book: A Surprise Gift from Your Photos

Updated Nov 2026 · 7 min read
Olga, designer at ReMeet, 10 years in editorial design

An anniversary photo book pulls together years of photos from dating, the wedding, trips, and everyday life. Share a Google Photos album with everything, and I sort it into chapters. Great as a surprise from one partner to the other, or from kids to parents. From $29.

An anniversary is one of those moments where you want to say something bigger than a card. A photo book does that. It takes the thousands of photos scattered across phones, laptops, and old iCloud accounts and turns them into a single, designed object that tells the story of your years together.

The problem is that gathering and sorting those photos takes forever. You have pictures from five different phones, two cloud accounts, and a folder on a laptop from 2017. Some of the best photos are screenshots from text messages. Some are blurry but irreplaceable.

I handle all of that. You share whatever you have, in whatever state it's in, and I build the book.

What to include in an anniversary book

The best anniversary books cover the full arc, not just the highlights. Here's what I typically pull from when designing one:

  • Dating photos. The earliest ones. First trips, first apartment, random selfies from before anyone thought to take good pictures.
  • The wedding. If you have professional wedding photos, they go in. If you only have phone shots from the reception, those work too.
  • Honeymoon and trips. Travel photos tell a story on their own. Each trip gets a few pages.
  • Yearly milestones. New house, new pet, new city, job change, birthday parties. The moments that mark the years.
  • Everyday moments. The kitchen at the old apartment. Sunday mornings. The dog on the couch. These are the photos that hit hardest ten years later.

You don't need to organize any of this. Send me everything and I sort it by date, pick the strongest photos, and build the chapters. If you want certain photos in or out, just tell me after you see the first draft.

How to gather photos secretly

If this is a surprise gift, the tricky part is collecting photos without the other person noticing. Here's how most people handle it:

  1. Create a private Google Photos album. Copy photos from your shared albums, your camera roll, and your old phone backups into this new album. Only you can see it.
  2. Ask family and friends. Text your closest people and ask them to share their favorite photos of you as a couple. Parents and siblings often have pictures you forgot existed. Have them add photos to a shared album that only you manage.
  3. Check old devices. That retired iPhone in the drawer, the laptop backup, the USB stick from 2019. Old photos are gold for anniversary books.
  4. Don't worry about duplicates. If the same photo comes from three different people, I remove the extras. Send everything.

All proofs and communication go through your email, so the other person won't see anything until you hand them the finished book.

Organizing by year vs. by theme

There are two good ways to structure an anniversary book, and I can do either one:

StructureBest forHow it reads
By year5+ years of photos, lots of varietyA timeline. Each year gets a section. You watch the story unfold.
By themeFewer years or a focused collectionChapters like "Travel," "Home," "Family," "Just Us." Photos grouped by what they show, not when they were taken.

For longer relationships with lots of photos, chronological usually works better. It gives the book a natural arc. For a 1st or 5th anniversary with fewer photos, thematic chapters keep the pages full and varied.

Tell me which you prefer, or leave it up to me. I'll pick the structure that works best for the photos you share. You can always change it after the first draft.

For parents' anniversary: a gift from the kids

Some of the best anniversary books I design are from adult children to their parents. The parents have been together 25 or 30 years. The kids gather photos from aunts, uncles, grandparents, and their own camera rolls.

Here's a good approach:

  • One person takes the lead. One sibling creates a shared Google Photos album and sends the link to everyone who might have photos.
  • Set a deadline. Give people a week to add their photos. After that, send me the album link.
  • Include scanned prints. Old wedding photos, vacation snapshots from the 1990s, holiday cards. Take a picture of them with your phone or scan them. I place them in the book alongside the digital photos.
  • Order copies for everyone. Extra printed copies are $69 each. Each sibling gets a copy, the parents get the original.

A milestone birthday book works the same way. The collection process is identical.

Frequently asked questions

Can I combine photos from different phones and accounts?

Yes. Share multiple Google Photos albums or iCloud links, one from each phone or account. I merge them and sort everything by date. You can also drop photos from an old hard drive or scanned prints into a shared Google Drive folder.

What if our early photos are low quality?

Old phone photos and scanned prints still work. I adjust cropping and layout to make them look their best. A grainy photo from your first date has more emotional weight than a sharp photo of a parking lot. I keep what matters and skip what doesn't.

How do I keep it a surprise?

Create a separate Google Photos album and copy the photos you want to include. Ask family members to share their photos to a private album only you can see. All communication goes through your email, so the other person won't see anything until you hand them the finished book.
