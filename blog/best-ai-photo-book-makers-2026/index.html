5 Best AI Photo Book Makers in 2026

September 2026 · 8 min read

Making a photo book used to mean spending hours dragging photos into templates. Now, several services use AI to do the heavy lifting — selecting your best photos, arranging layouts, and even writing captions. But not all AI photo book makers are created equal. Some use AI for basic auto-fill. Others use it for genuine curation and design.

We tested five AI-powered photo book services to see how they compare on design quality, ease of use, pricing, and the actual intelligence of their AI. Here is what we found.

1. Remeet

What it is: A done-for-you photo book service where AI curates your photos and a human designer reviews the result.
Price: $69 – $199
Best for: People with large photo libraries who want a premium, editorial-quality book without doing any design work.

What the AI does

Remeet's AI scans your entire photo library — thousands of images at once — and handles the full pipeline: quality scoring (blur, exposure, composition, face detection), duplicate removal, event-based grouping, layout design with pacing and photo pairing, and caption suggestions. After the AI creates an initial design, a human designer reviews and refines it before you see a proof.

Pros

  • Most comprehensive AI pipeline of any service — handles curation, layout, and captioning end-to-end
  • Human designer reviews every book, catching what AI misses
  • Handles very large libraries (10,000+ photos) without overwhelming you
  • Editorial-quality design with professional pacing and typography
  • Premium paper and printing quality

Cons

  • More expensive than DIY or auto-generated options
  • Less hands-on control than a full DIY editor
  • Turnaround takes 72 hours for the proof (not instant)
  • Newer service — smaller track record than established players

Our take: If you have thousands of photos and want a book that looks like a designer made it — because one did — Remeet is the best option. The price reflects the human review step, which genuinely improves the result. Not ideal if you want to hand-place every photo yourself.

2. Chatbooks

What it is: An app that auto-generates monthly photo books from your camera roll or Instagram.
Price: $10 – $40
Best for: Casual monthly snapshots. Parents who want a quick, affordable record of everyday moments.

What the AI does

Chatbooks connects to your photo sources (camera roll, Instagram, Facebook) and automatically pulls in recent photos. Its AI does basic photo selection — removing screenshots, duplicates, and very dark or blurry images — and drops the rest into simple templates in chronological order. You can swap photos or rearrange pages before ordering.

Pros

  • Extremely easy — a book can be ready in under 5 minutes
  • Very affordable, especially for softcover monthly books
  • Auto-subscription option creates books every month without effort
  • Good mobile app experience
  • Fast shipping (5-7 business days)

Cons

  • AI is basic — it filters out bad photos but does not truly curate
  • No intelligent layout design; photos go into fixed grid templates
  • No narrative structure, chapter organization, or pacing
  • Print quality is decent but not premium
  • Limited customization options

Our take: Chatbooks is the fastest and cheapest option on this list. If you want a simple printed record of the month's photos and you do not care about editorial design, it is hard to beat. But the AI does not actually design anything — it is more of a filter-and-fill system. Think of it as printing your camera roll, minus the junk.

3. Mixbook Story Mode

What it is: An AI-assisted mode within Mixbook's DIY photo book editor that suggests layouts and auto-places photos.
Price: $40 – $130
Best for: People who want some AI help but also want control over the final design.

What the AI does

Mixbook's Story Mode analyzes your uploaded photos and suggests page layouts, photo placement, and color-coordinated backgrounds. It groups photos by date and visual similarity, suggests which images work best as full-page spreads versus smaller grid elements, and auto-fills pages with a starting layout. You then have full access to Mixbook's editor to adjust everything.

Pros

  • Best of both worlds — AI starts the design, you refine it
  • Excellent drag-and-drop editor for fine-tuning
  • Good variety of templates and design styles
  • Solid print quality with multiple paper and cover options
  • Reasonable pricing with frequent sales and promotions

Cons

  • AI suggestions are a starting point, not a finished design — you still spend time editing
  • Photo curation is limited — it does not deeply analyze quality or remove duplicates
  • No caption generation
  • Can feel overwhelming with too many customization options
  • Story Mode works best with pre-selected photos, not a full library dump

Our take: Mixbook Story Mode is the best option for people who enjoy the design process but want a smarter starting point than a blank page. The AI cuts your layout time roughly in half. But you still need to do significant work — selecting your best photos beforehand, rearranging suggestions, and tweaking layouts. If you genuinely enjoy that process, this is excellent. If you want hands-off, look elsewhere.

4. Google Photos Books

What it is: A built-in photo book feature within Google Photos that auto-generates simple books from your albums.
Price: $15 – $30
Best for: Quick, simple books from a specific trip or event already organized in Google Photos.

What the AI does

Google Photos uses its AI to suggest photo books based on your albums, trips, or time periods. It automatically selects photos it considers "best" from the set (favoring sharp, well-exposed images with faces), drops them into clean minimal layouts, and can auto-generate captions using location data. The AI leverages Google's powerful image recognition — the same tech behind Google Photos search — to identify highlights.

Pros

  • Seamless if your photos are already in Google Photos
  • Google's image AI is genuinely excellent at identifying good photos
  • Very affordable pricing
  • Clean, minimal design aesthetic
  • Location-based auto-captioning is useful
  • Proactive suggestions — Google surfaces book ideas for you

Cons

  • Very limited layout options — a handful of fixed templates
  • No pacing, narrative structure, or chapter organization
  • Softcover only, limited size options
  • Print quality is basic (adequate but not premium)
  • Editing tools are minimal — hard to fine-tune layouts
  • Only works with photos stored in Google Photos

Our take: Google Photos Books is the hidden gem for people who just want a simple, clean book from a specific album without any fuss. The photo selection AI is legitimately good — Google has been doing image analysis longer than almost anyone. But the book itself is basic: limited layouts, softcover, no premium finishing. For a quick vacation book or a simple gift, it is surprisingly solid for the price. For anything you want to display on a coffee table, the quality falls short.

5. Apple Photos Books

What it is: A built-in photo book creator in macOS Photos app that uses Apple Intelligence to suggest layouts.
Price: $15 – $50
Best for: Apple ecosystem users who want a straightforward book creation experience without leaving the Photos app.

What the AI does

Apple's photo book feature uses on-device AI to analyze your photo library and suggest book projects. It identifies key moments, selects photos with good faces and composition, and auto-fills pages with varied layouts. Apple Intelligence can suggest thematic groupings and automatically enhance photos before placing them. The system works entirely on-device, which means your photos never leave your Mac during the AI analysis phase.

Pros

  • Deep integration with Apple Photos — uses your existing albums, favorites, and memories
  • On-device AI processing keeps photos private
  • Good variety of layout templates and themes
  • Decent print quality with hardcover and softcover options
  • Automatic photo enhancement (lighting, color correction)
  • No additional app or account needed if you use a Mac

Cons

  • Mac only — no Windows, no web, no mobile-first workflow
  • AI curation is moderate — better than random but not deeply intelligent
  • Layout suggestions can feel repetitive across pages
  • No caption generation or storytelling features
  • The editor is functional but not as polished as dedicated services
  • Pricing is moderate — not the cheapest, not the best quality for the price

Our take: Apple Photos Books is a solid option if you live in the Apple ecosystem and want something better than Google Photos without installing another app. The privacy angle is real — your photos stay on your device during analysis. But the AI is not dramatically smarter than Google's in terms of book design, and the Mac-only requirement limits accessibility. A good middle ground between Google's simplicity and Mixbook's complexity.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Chatbooks or Google Photos if you want something quick and affordable. Both are great for casual books — a month of family photos, a single trip, a simple gift. Chatbooks wins on ease, Google wins on photo selection smarts.

Choose Mixbook Story Mode if you enjoy the design process and want AI to give you a head start. You will still spend time customizing, but you start from a much better place than a blank canvas.

Choose Apple Photos if you are already in the Apple ecosystem and privacy matters to you. It is a solid all-rounder that does not require installing anything new.

Choose Remeet if you have a large photo library, you value design quality, and you do not want to spend hours in an editor. The combination of deep AI curation and human review produces results that the other services cannot match — but it costs more, and you give up granular control.

There is no single best choice for everyone. The right service depends on how many photos you have, how much time you want to spend, what quality level you expect, and what you are willing to pay. The good news: AI has made every option on this list better than the fully manual tools of five years ago.
