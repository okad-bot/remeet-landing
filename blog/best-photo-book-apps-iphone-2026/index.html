7 Best Photo Book Apps for iPhone in 2026

November 2026 · 5 min read

Your iPhone has thousands of photos. Turning them into a photo book used to mean sitting at a computer for hours. Now there are apps that do most of the work on your phone. Some use AI to pick your best shots and arrange them. Others give you a simple editor. A few just auto-generate books from your camera roll every month.

We tested seven photo book apps that work on iPhone. Here is how they compare on ease of use, design quality, AI features, and price.

1. Remeet

What it is: An AI-powered photo book service. Upload photos from your iPhone, and Remeet's AI handles everything: scoring quality, removing duplicates, grouping by event, designing layouts, and suggesting captions. A human designer reviews the result before you see a proof.
Price: $29 (30 pages) – $79 (100 pages)
Best for: People with thousands of iPhone photos who want a designer-quality book without spending time in an editor.

Remeet works well with large iPhone libraries because the AI is built for volume. Upload 1,000 or 5,000 photos and it figures out which ones matter: sharp faces, good lighting, meaningful moments. It removes the screenshots, the accidental shots, and the seven nearly identical photos from the same angle. Then it builds a book with pacing, variety, and photo pairing that actually looks designed.

The result is a digital photo book you can view on any device or get printed. The whole process takes about five minutes of your time. You get a proof in 72 hours, review it, request changes in plain language, and approve when it looks right.

Pick Remeet if your camera roll is a mess and you want someone (well, an AI plus a human) to make sense of it. Not the right fit if you want to drag and drop every photo yourself.

2. Chatbooks

What it is: An app that auto-generates monthly photo books from your camera roll.
Price: ~$10/month (softcover) – $40 (hardcover)
Best for: Busy parents who want photo books to happen automatically.

Chatbooks connects to your iPhone camera roll and pulls in recent photos every month. Its AI filters out screenshots, blurry shots, and duplicates, then drops the rest into clean grid templates in chronological order. A book shows up at your door. You can swap photos or reorder pages before it ships, but most people don't bother. That is the whole point.

Pick Chatbooks if you want consistency over curation. A book a month, no decisions required. The design is basic, but after a year you have twelve volumes on the shelf without having thought about it.

3. Mixbook

What it is: A photo book editor with AI-assisted layout suggestions.
Price: ~$25 – $130
Best for: People who enjoy designing but want a smart starting point.

Mixbook's iPhone app lets you upload photos and get AI-generated layout suggestions. It groups your photos by date and visual similarity, proposes which images work as full-page spreads versus smaller elements, and auto-fills pages. You keep full control to rearrange, swap, and tweak everything. The templates are well-designed and print quality is solid.

Pick Mixbook if you want the creative process but not the blank-page problem. The AI saves you the first hour of layout work, and the editor is one of the best on mobile.

4. Google Photos

What it is: A built-in photo book feature inside the Google Photos app.
Price: ~$15 (softcover) – $30 (hardcover)
Best for: Quick books from a specific trip or event.

If you back up photos to Google Photos, you have probably seen it suggest a photo book after a vacation or birthday. The AI groups your photos by location and date, picks what it thinks are the highlights, and proposes a simple book. You can edit it in-app before ordering a print. Design is free. You only pay when you order.

Google's photo recognition AI is genuinely strong at identifying your best shots. But the layouts are minimal and the design options are limited. It works best for a focused set of photos from one event, not for your entire camera roll.

Pick Google Photos if you already use it and want a quick, affordable book from a trip. Do not expect editorial design.

5. Shutterfly

What it is: The mainstream photo book app. Simple editor, basic auto-fill, frequent sales.
Price: ~$10 – $80 (heavily coupon-dependent)
Best for: Budget buyers willing to wait for a promotion.

Shutterfly's iPhone app has a functional editor with hundreds of templates. Upload your photos, pick a theme, and the app auto-fills pages. The AI is minimal: it places photos in slots but does not score quality, remove duplicates, or make design decisions. You do the curation. Where Shutterfly wins is price: with their frequent 40-60% off sales, a hardcover book can cost under $15.

Pick Shutterfly if you are price-sensitive and already know which photos you want in the book. The app does the assembling. You do the thinking.

6. Snapfish

What it is: A budget photo book app similar to Shutterfly.
Price: ~$10 – $50
Best for: The cheapest possible photo book.

Snapfish works almost identically to Shutterfly: upload photos, pick a template, auto-fill, order. The AI does little beyond placing images in slots. Print quality is acceptable for casual albums. The main draw is regular promotions that make small books nearly free (just pay shipping). Design tools on the iPhone app are more limited than Shutterfly's, but for a simple book at rock-bottom price, it gets the job done.

Pick Snapfish if price is the only thing that matters and you are fine with basic design.

7. Apple Photos

What it is: The built-in Photos app on your iPhone and Mac, with book creation features.
Price: ~$15 – $50
Best for: People deep in the Apple ecosystem who want zero extra apps.

Apple Photos uses on-device intelligence to suggest Memories and photo groupings, which you can turn into printed books through partner services like Motif. The AI leverages face recognition, location data, and scene detection that already runs on your iPhone. If you have organized albums in Photos, turning one into a book is straightforward. Design quality sits between Google Photos and Mixbook: cleaner than a basic auto-fill, less refined than a dedicated editor.

Pick Apple Photos if you do not want another app and your photos are already well-organized in Apple's ecosystem. The convenience is the value, not the design polish.

How to choose

  1. How many photos? Thousands in your camera roll: Remeet or Chatbooks. A specific album of 50-200: any app works.
  2. How much time? Zero: Remeet or Chatbooks. Some: Mixbook or Google Photos. Full DIY: Shutterfly.
  3. Budget? Under $15: Snapfish or Shutterfly with a coupon. $15-30: Google Photos or Apple Photos. $29-79: Remeet.
  4. Digital or print? Remeet delivers a digital book (printable too). Everyone else is print-first.
  5. One-time or recurring? Monthly auto-books: Chatbooks. One special book: Remeet, Mixbook, or Artifact Uprising.
