Best Photo Book Services in 2026 — An Honest Comparison

September 2026 · 8 min read

Creating a photo book should be simple: pick your photos, get a beautiful book. But most services make you do all the design work yourself — dragging photos into templates, adjusting layouts, writing captions. It takes hours.

We compared five popular photo book services to help you decide which one is right for your photos, your time, and your budget.

What we compared

We evaluated each service on four criteria that matter most to busy families:

  • Design quality — Does the final book look professionally designed or like a DIY template?
  • Ease of use — How much time and effort does it take from start to finished book?
  • Pricing — What do you actually pay for a hardcover book with 60-80 pages?
  • Turnaround — How long from placing the order to holding the book?

Artifact Uprising

Artifact Uprising makes beautiful, premium-quality books. The paper quality is excellent, and the materials feel luxurious. However, you're the designer. You choose every layout, place every photo, and write every caption yourself.

Best for: People who enjoy the design process and have time to spend on it.

The catch: Most people start a project and never finish it. The editor is powerful but complex, and a 60-page book can easily take 4-6 hours to design.

Chatbooks

Chatbooks is the fastest, most affordable option. It connects to your Instagram or camera roll and auto-generates a book. The trade-off is design quality — layouts are basic, there's minimal curation, and books can feel like a printed feed rather than a story.

Best for: Quick, affordable snapshots. Monthly series for casual documentation.

The catch: No editorial design. No chapter structure. No curation of your best photos. What you get is every photo in order, with basic borders.

Mixbook

Mixbook offers a solid middle ground. The templates are better than Shutterfly's, the editor is more intuitive, and there's more creative freedom. They frequently run sales, making it reasonably affordable.

Best for: People who want more design control than Chatbooks but less complexity than Artifact Uprising.

The catch: You still need to select photos, choose layouts, and place everything yourself. Budget 2-3 hours for a good result.

Shutterfly

Shutterfly is the biggest name in the space. They offer almost everything — books, cards, gifts, prints. Photo books are template-based with a drag-and-drop editor. Quality is decent for the price, especially during their frequent 40-50% off sales.

Best for: Budget-conscious families who want a physical book and are comfortable with template editors.

The catch: Design options feel dated compared to newer services. The editor can be clunky. Without a sale, pricing isn't competitive.

Remeet

Remeet takes a fundamentally different approach: you share your photos, and a team of designers creates an editorial-quality book for you. There's no editor to learn, no templates to choose, no layouts to arrange. You just share a Google Photos album or Drive folder and get a designed proof in 72 hours.

The result looks more like a magazine or coffee-table book than a typical photo book — with curated photo selection, chapter titles, captions, and thoughtful page compositions.

Best for: People with thousands of photos who want a beautiful book but don't have time (or desire) to design one themselves.

The catch: At $199 for a printed book, it's the most expensive option on this list. But you're paying for professional design work, not just printing.

So which one should you choose?

If you want the cheapest option: Chatbooks ($10-15/book for softcover)

If you enjoy designing: Artifact Uprising or Mixbook

If you want it done for you: Remeet

If you want the biggest selection of products: Shutterfly

The real question isn't which service is "best" — it's what you value more: your time or your budget. The DIY services save money but cost hours of work. A done-for-you service like Remeet costs more but gives you back that time.

For most busy families, the bigger risk isn't spending too much on a photo book — it's never making one at all. Thousands of photos sit on phones, unseen and unshared, because the project feels too big to start.

Whatever you choose, the best photo book is the one that actually gets made.
