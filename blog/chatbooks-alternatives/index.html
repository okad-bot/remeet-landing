5 Chatbooks Alternatives If You Want More Than a Small Square Book

ReMeet is a done-for-you alternative to Chatbooks. Send a link to your photos and a designer builds the book from $79 for up to 100 pages: first proof in 72 hours, two rounds of edits, printed hardcover.

November 2026 · 9 min read
Written by Olga Miller, founder of ReMeet

Chatbooks is one of the simplest ways to turn photos into a book. Connect your Instagram or Google Photos, pick a date range, and you get a small, affordable photo book in the mail. For casual monthly snapshots, it's hard to beat.

But many people eventually want more. A bigger book. Better layouts. Curated photos instead of every shot in order. Chapter titles and captions that tell a story, not just print a feed.

If you've outgrown Chatbooks - or you're looking for an alternative from the start - here are five services worth considering. Each offers something different, and the right one depends on whether you value your time, your budget, or your creative control.

What Chatbooks does well (and where it falls short)

Before comparing alternatives, it's worth being honest about what Chatbooks gets right.

Strengths: It's fast. It's cheap ($10-15 for a softcover). The subscription model is clever - your phone's camera roll automatically becomes a book every month. There's almost no friction.

Limitations: Books are small format (typically 6x6 or 8x8). Layouts are basic - one photo per page, maybe two. There's no photo curation, no duplicate removal, no chapter structure. Every photo goes in, in order. The result feels less like a designed book and more like a printed camera roll.

1. Mixbook - Best for creative control

If the main reason you're leaving Chatbooks is that you want more design freedom, Mixbook is probably your best option. Its editor is the most intuitive of any DIY service, with hundreds of themes, flexible layouts, and the ability to move, resize, and layer photos exactly where you want them.

What you get: Better templates than Chatbooks (by far), multiple book sizes including large-format landscape and portrait, and good print quality. Mixbook also launched an AI Story Mode that auto-generates a starting layout - a nice time saver, though you'll still want to edit it.

The trade-off: You're the designer. Selecting photos, arranging layouts, adjusting cropping, and writing any text is on you. A 60-80 page book typically takes 2-3 hours. Pricing is higher than Chatbooks - expect $80-130 for an 80-page hardcover, though 40-50% sales are frequent.

Best for: People who enjoy the creative process and want a book that feels personally designed.

For a head-to-head comparison of the two biggest DIY services, see Shutterfly vs Mixbook. For more alternatives in this space, see Mixbook Alternatives.

2. Shutterfly - Best for budget and product variety

Shutterfly is the biggest name in the photo book space, and for good reason. It offers the widest range of products - not just books, but cards, calendars, mugs, blankets, and more. If you want to do more with your photos than just make a book, Shutterfly's ecosystem is unmatched.

What you get: A template-based editor with drag-and-drop functionality, multiple book sizes, and deep discounts. Shutterfly runs sales constantly - 40-50% off is normal, and you'll sometimes see free 8x8 books (you pay shipping). An 80-page hardcover runs $50-90 at sale price.

The trade-off: The editor feels dated compared to Mixbook. Templates are less modern, and the design options can feel cluttered. Without a sale, pricing isn't competitive. You still need to sort, upload, and arrange everything yourself.

Best for: Budget-conscious families who want a physical book and are comfortable with a template editor. For a direct comparison, see ReMeet vs Shutterfly.

3. Artifact Uprising - Best for premium quality

Artifact Uprising is the luxury end of the market. The paper stock is thick and archival. The covers are real linen or leather. The whole package feels like something you'd find in a design store, not a drugstore.

What you get: The best print quality of any consumer photo book service. Beautiful paper, precise color reproduction, and binding that lasts. If you're making a wedding book, a memorial book, or something that needs to last decades, the materials justify the price.

The trade-off: It's expensive. An 80-page hardcover runs $150-220. And like every other DIY service, you're responsible for every design decision. The editor is powerful but complex. Most people underestimate the time commitment - plan for 4-6 hours on a 60-page book. Many projects get started and never finished.

Best for: People who value premium materials and enjoy designing. Heirloom-quality books for major life events.

4. Snapfish - Cheapest DIY alternative

If Chatbooks' low price was the main appeal and you just want a bigger, slightly better book without spending much more, Snapfish is worth a look. It's one of the cheapest photo book services available, especially during its frequent sales.

What you get: Softcover books starting around $8, hardcovers from $40-70 for 80 pages. Multiple sizes, a straightforward template editor, and sales that regularly top 50% off. Snapfish covers the basics at a low price.

The trade-off: Design quality is basic. The templates are functional but not particularly modern. Print quality is acceptable for the price but noticeably behind Mixbook or Artifact Uprising. Like Shutterfly, the editor requires you to handle photo selection, layout, and cropping yourself.

Best for: People who want the cheapest possible upgrade from Chatbooks' format and don't mind doing the design work. See also ReMeet vs Chatbooks for a more detailed comparison.

Which Chatbooks alternative is right for you?

If you want more design control: Mixbook. Best editor, best templates, good balance of flexibility and guidance.

If you want the cheapest upgrade: Snapfish or Shutterfly during a sale. Bigger books, more formats, low prices.

If you want premium quality for a special occasion: Artifact Uprising. Nobody beats the materials.

If you don't want to design at all: ReMeet. Share your photos, get a professionally designed book in 72 hours.

The real question: design it yourself, or have it done?

Most people searching for Chatbooks alternatives are looking for better quality. But better quality in the DIY world comes with more work. More templates to choose from means more decisions. More layout options means more time arranging. More features means a steeper learning curve.

This is why the most common outcome with DIY photo book services isn't a bad book - it's no book at all. The project gets started, sits in a cart for weeks, and quietly expires. For more on this, see Photo Book Layout Tips and our Complete Guide to Making a Photo Book.

If what you really want is a finished book - one that looks beautiful, tells your year's story, and actually sits on your shelf - the fastest path isn't a better editor. It's someone else doing the work.
