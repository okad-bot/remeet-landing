Cheapest Photo Book Services in 2026: Prices Compared Side by Side

November 2026 · 10 min read

You have a thousand photos on your phone and you want them in a book. The obvious first question: what is the cheapest way to do it?

We compared eight of the most popular photo book services on price, what you actually get for that price, and how much of your own time you need to spend. The answer to "which is cheapest" depends on whether you count only the dollars you pay or also the hours you invest.

The true cost: price + your time

Every photo book that is not done for you requires the same invisible work. You sort through your camera roll. You pick which photos to include. You upload them. You drag them into a template. You crop the ones that don't fit. You fix the ones where someone's head is cut off. You reorder pages. You add text. You check every page before ordering.

For most people, that process takes two to four hours. For parents sorting through a year of photos, it can take an entire weekend.

If your time is worth $30 an hour (roughly the median hourly wage in the US), the real cost of a "cheap" photo book changes dramatically.

What "cheap" actually looks like

There's a quality difference behind the price difference, and it matters.

Template-based services ($3-$15)

Walgreens, Snapfish, and Shutterfly give you a grid of rectangles and you fill them with photos. The layout is the same on every page. There's no photo curation: if you upload 200 photos including 30 near-duplicates and 15 blurry shots, they all go into the book unless you manually remove them. Cropping is automatic and often cuts off heads, hands, or important details at the edges. The result looks like what it is: a template filled with photos.

AI-assisted services ($15-$30)

Google Photos and Mixbook use algorithms to suggest which photos to include and how to arrange them. The result is better than a blank template, but you still need to review every page, swap photos the AI chose poorly, fix cropping, and reorder sections that don't flow. Chatbooks is the fastest in this group because it auto-pulls from your phone, but the books are small (6x6 inches) and the design options are limited.

Premium DIY ($19-$59+)

Artifact Uprising offers the best paper quality and printing of any service on this list. The books are genuinely beautiful objects. But you design every page yourself from a blank canvas, which takes real time and real design skill to get right. If you enjoy the design process, it's worth it. If you just want the book done, it's the most expensive option on the list.

Done-for-you ($29-$79)

Remeet is the only service here where you send a link to your photo album and receive a finished, designed book without touching an editor. The AI selects and scores your best photos, removes duplicates and blurry shots, groups images by date and event, and builds the layout. Then a human designer reviews every page: checking crops, adjusting text placement, and making sure no face is cut off. The full list of what's included is on the What We Handle page.

When the budget option makes sense

Not every photo book needs to be a masterpiece. Sometimes the cheapest sticker price is the right call.

  • A quick gift you need by tomorrow. Walgreens offers same-day pickup. If you need a physical book in your hands tonight, nothing else comes close.
  • A small book from a single event. Twenty photos from a birthday party don't need professional curation. Upload, fill a template, order. Done.
  • You genuinely enjoy designing. Some people find photo book editing relaxing. If you like spending a Sunday arranging photos and tweaking layouts, Artifact Uprising or Mixbook will reward that effort with a beautiful result.
  • Bulk simple prints. If you need 20 identical copies of a basic photo booklet for a school event, Shutterfly's volume pricing wins.

When done-for-you is actually cheaper

In other situations, spending $29 on Remeet saves both money and sanity.

  • A year of family photos. You have 3,000 photos on your phone from the last twelve months. Sorting through them to pick the best 100 takes most people four to six hours. With Remeet, you share a link and get a proof in 72 hours.
  • A baby's first year. There are 8,000 photos and they all look important. The AI identifies the best shots, removes the hundreds of near-identical frames, and organizes everything by month. You would never finish this book on your own, and that is exactly why most DIY photo book projects get abandoned halfway through.
  • A gift for grandparents. You want it to look good but you don't have time to learn a design editor. Remeet delivers a finished PDF you can preview, edit, and approve in minutes.
  • You've tried before and quit. If you've started a Shutterfly or Mixbook project and abandoned it after page 12 because the cropping was wrong and every page looked the same, a done-for-you service exists precisely for you.

The abandonment problem

Photo book projects have a high dropout rate. People start with good intentions, spend an hour uploading and arranging, realize how much work is left, and close the tab. The project sits in their cart for months, then expires. The cheapest photo book is the one that actually gets finished.
