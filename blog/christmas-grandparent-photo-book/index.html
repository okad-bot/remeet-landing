Who this is for

Parents making a gift for the grandparents. You have hundreds of photos of the kids on your phone. The grandparents want to see them, but they're not going to scroll through Google Photos. A book on the coffee table gets opened every day.

Families with grandparents in different households. Design the book once. Order copies for each address. Same book, same price per copy, different shipping destinations. Everyone gets the same gift without anyone knowing you only spent five minutes on it.

Long-distance grandparents. When grandparents live far away, a photo book fills in the months between visits. The Tuesday morning pancakes. The first bike ride. The backyard fort. These are the moments they miss, and a book brings them back.

What to include

You don't need to decide this. I'll pick the best photos and organize them. But if you want to guide the selection, here's what works well in grandparent books:

  • Milestones. First day of school. Lost teeth. Birthdays. Grandparents want to see growth.
  • Everyday moments. Homework at the kitchen table. Siblings on the couch. The baby napping. These are the photos that trigger the deepest response in print.
  • Visit photos and FaceTime screenshots. Cooking together, reading bedtime stories, walking to the park. For long-distance grandparents, video call screenshots are powerful - they document the effort to stay connected.
  • Kid artwork and handwriting. Photograph the best drawings. If the kids can write, include a note to grandma. Handwriting changes fast, and grandparents notice.
  • Captions in the kids' own words. Ask each child: "What's your favorite thing about Grandma?" Write their answers exactly as they say them. A five-year-old's grammar is worth more than any professional caption.
