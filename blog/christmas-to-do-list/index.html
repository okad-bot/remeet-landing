Christmas To-Do List 2026: What to Do Before December

November 2026 · 10 min read
Written by Olga Miller, founder of ReMeet

Start in November. This week-by-week checklist covers gifts, decorations, food, travel, and all the small tasks that pile up before December 25. Order personalized gifts like photo books by December 1 to ensure delivery before Christmas.

Christmas is one of those holidays that feels comfortably far away until it suddenly isn’t. One day you’re thinking “I have plenty of time,” and the next you’re standing in a crowded store on December 23 wondering how it all went sideways. The gifts aren’t wrapped, the fridge is empty, and you just remembered your nephew.

The fix is straightforward: start early and work from a real list. Not a vague mental list that shifts every time you think about it, but an actual week-by-week plan that covers gifts, food, decorations, travel, and all the small things that pile up when you ignore them.

Here is a complete Christmas to-do list for 2026, broken down by deadline. Use it as a checklist. Skip the parts that don’t apply. The goal is not perfection - the goal is arriving at Christmas Day feeling calm instead of frantic.

By November 30: set the foundation

November is for planning. You’re not buying much yet (with a few exceptions). You’re getting organized so every decision in December is easier and faster.

Set your Christmas budget

Before you spend a dollar, decide how much you want to spend in total. Then break it down:

  • Gifts - set a per-person amount
  • Food and drinks for holiday meals and gatherings
  • Decorations - new items for this year
  • Christmas cards and postage
  • Wrapping supplies - paper, ribbon, tape, gift bags
  • Travel - flights, gas, hotels
  • Tips and small gifts for teachers, mail carriers, service providers
  • Charity - donations, toy drives, food bank contributions

People consistently underestimate the non-gift costs. Cards, wrapping paper, extra food for guests, a bottle of wine to bring to a party - these add up. Build in a 10-15% buffer for things you forgot.

Build your complete gift list

Write down every person you need to buy for. Not just immediate family, but extended family, friends who do gift exchanges, kids’ teachers, neighbors, coworkers, your dog sitter. The list is always longer than you think.

Next to each name, write your budget and at least one gift idea. Don’t overthink it yet - you’ll refine over the coming weeks. The point is to see the full picture now instead of discovering on December 20 that you forgot someone.

Order personalized gifts now

This is the one exception to “don’t buy anything yet.” Personalized gifts need production time, and they’re some of the most meaningful presents you can give. Custom photo books, engraved jewelry, monogrammed items, custom prints - none of these can be ordered on December 18 and arrive by Christmas.

A Christmas photo book is a great example. A book from ReMeet takes 72 hours for design, then you review the proof, then it goes to print and ships. That’s roughly 10-14 days from order to delivery. Order by the first week of December to be safe - or order now and cross it off the list entirely.

Book travel

If you’re flying or driving long distance for Christmas, book now. Flight prices climb steeply from mid-November onward. Hotels near family fill up. The earlier you book, the more options you have and the less you’ll pay.

Check your decoration inventory

Pull out last year’s decorations from storage. What still works? What’s broken, missing, or burned out? Make a list of what you need so you can pick things up gradually instead of panic-buying everything on December 10.

Shop Black Friday and Cyber Monday

November 27 (Black Friday) and November 30 (Cyber Monday) are the best deal days of the year. Cross off as many gifts as you can during these sales. If you built your gift list earlier in the month, you’ll know exactly what to look for instead of impulse-buying.

Choose your charitable giving

Decide which charities, toy drives, or food banks you want to support this year. Many organizations have November deadlines for holiday programs like Toys for Tots or Angel Tree. If your workplace does a giving drive, sign up now.

November checklist

  • Set total Christmas budget with categories
  • Write complete gift list (name + budget + idea)
  • Order personalized gifts (photo books, custom items)
  • Book flights, hotels, or rental cars
  • Inventory decorations and note replacements
  • Shop Black Friday / Cyber Monday deals
  • Sign up for charity drives and donations
  • Decide: hosting or traveling this year?

First week of December (Dec 1-7): shop, decorate, send

November was planning. December is doing. This first week is when you shift from lists to action.

Complete your gift shopping

Turn those rough ideas from your gift list into actual purchases. Order online where you can - most retailers still offer standard shipping that arrives well before Christmas. For anything you need to buy in person, make a store-by-store list so you can knock it out in one or two trips instead of five scattered ones.

Still stuck on what to give someone? A photo book works for almost anyone: parents, grandparents, partners, friends. It’s personal, it’s permanent, and it doesn’t end up in a drawer.

Put up decorations

The first week of December is the sweet spot. Early enough to enjoy the decorations for most of the month. Late enough that a real tree won’t be dropping needles by Christmas Day. If you’re getting a real tree, most lots open by December 1. Set up outdoor lights while the weather is still manageable.

Send Christmas cards

If you send cards, the first week of December is when they should go out. This gives them time to arrive before the postal system gets overwhelmed mid-month. For international cards, ideally they went out in late November, but send them now if you haven’t.

Plan your Christmas meals

Decide what you’re making for Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. Write full menus, then create ingredient lists. For anything nonperishable - canned goods, baking supplies, spices, dried pasta - buy it this week.

If you’re hosting, nail down the details:

  • How many guests are coming?
  • Any dietary restrictions or allergies?
  • What can be made ahead and frozen?
  • Do you have enough plates, glasses, and chairs?
  • Are guests bringing dishes? Assign items to avoid duplicates.

RSVP to holiday parties

Check your calendar for invitations you haven’t responded to. RSVP now so hosts can plan. Add all events to your calendar so nothing overlaps and nothing gets forgotten.

Confirm personalized gift orders

If you ordered a custom photo book or other personalized item in November, check on its status. Make sure proofs are approved and orders are on track for delivery. If you haven’t ordered yet, this is your last comfortable window - after this week, you’re cutting it close.

First week of December checklist

  • Complete online gift orders
  • Make in-store shopping trips (consolidate by store)
  • Send Christmas cards
  • Put up tree, lights, and decorations
  • Plan menus and buy nonperishable ingredients
  • RSVP to all holiday party invitations
  • Confirm personalized gift orders are on track
  • Buy wrapping supplies (paper, tape, ribbon, tags)

Second week of December (Dec 8-14): wrap, bake, prepare

Most of your shopping should be done. This week is about wrapping up loose ends and getting ahead on food preparation.

Wrap your gifts

Don’t save wrapping for Christmas Eve. Set aside one evening, put on a movie or a podcast, and wrap everything at once. Label each gift clearly. Store wrapped presents somewhere children can’t find them.

If wrapping isn’t your thing, gift bags with tissue paper take 30 seconds per gift and look perfectly fine. No one judges the wrapping once the gift is open.

Start holiday baking

Many holiday cookies and desserts freeze well. Sugar cookies, gingerbread, biscotti, pie crusts, cookie dough - make them this week and freeze. This spreads the kitchen work across two weeks instead of cramming it all into the last few days.

Prep make-ahead dishes

Casseroles, cranberry sauce, bread rolls, soup, and many side dishes can be prepared and frozen now. On Christmas Day, you’ll pull them out, reheat, and serve. This is the single biggest stress reducer for anyone hosting.

Place final online orders

December 10-15 is typically the last window for standard shipping to arrive by December 24. After that, you’ll need expedited shipping (expensive) or in-store pickup. Mark the cutoff dates for your preferred retailers and don’t miss them.

Buy stocking stuffers

These are easy to forget. Small items: candy, lip balm, socks, mini games, gift cards, tiny books, art supplies for kids. Buy them all in one trip this week.

Confirm travel plans

Double-check flight times, hotel reservations, and driving routes. If you’re driving, start watching weather forecasts for your route. If someone is watching your house or pets while you’re away, confirm those arrangements now.

Clean and prepare guest spaces

If you’re hosting overnight guests, get the guest room ready this week instead of the day before. Clean sheets, fresh towels, an empty drawer or closet space, a phone charger on the nightstand. Small touches that make guests feel welcome.

Second week of December checklist

  • Wrap and label all purchased gifts
  • Bake and freeze holiday cookies and desserts
  • Prep and freeze make-ahead dishes
  • Place final online orders (check shipping deadlines)
  • Buy stocking stuffers
  • Confirm travel reservations
  • Clean and prepare guest rooms

Last week before Christmas (Dec 15-24): final stretch

If you’ve followed the earlier steps, this stretch should feel manageable. Most of the work is done. What’s left is the fresh food, the final details, and the fun part.

December 15-18: last-minute gifts and errands

Pick up any remaining in-store purchases. If you’re still missing a gift, this is your last chance for something thoughtful. After December 20, you’re limited to whatever is on the shelf at the store down the street.

Ideas for truly last-minute gifts: experience vouchers (dinner, concert tickets, spa day), a ReMeet digital photo book (order now, deliver the PDF proof as the gift - they can view it on a tablet or TV), or a heartfelt letter paired with a small meaningful item.

December 19-21: grocery shopping

Buy fresh ingredients: produce, meat, dairy, fresh herbs, fresh bread. Go early in the day - stores are crowded after 4 PM this week, and specific cuts of meat sell out. If you need something from a specialty store (a bakery, a butcher, a cheese shop), go on the 19th, not the 23rd.

December 22-23: cook and assemble

Pull frozen prep-ahead items from the freezer to thaw. Start anything that needs to cook slowly - braises, roasts that need marinating, bread that needs to rise overnight. Assemble cold dishes. Set the table if you want it done and off your mind.

December 24: final setup

Place gifts under the tree. Fill stockings. Set out anything for Santa if you have little ones. Do one final check: is there wrapping paper to clean up, an unwrapped gift hiding somewhere, a card you forgot to write?

Then sit down. Look at the tree. The hard part is over. You did all the work. Tomorrow is the payoff.

December 25: be present

Charge your phone and camera the night before - you’ll want photos of Christmas morning. Eat the food. Watch the kids tear open gifts. Let the dishes sit in the sink for a while. This is the day everything was building toward. Don’t spend it cleaning.

Last week checklist

  • Pick up remaining in-store gifts
  • Buy fresh groceries (go early in the day)
  • Thaw frozen prep-ahead dishes
  • Cook, assemble, and plate holiday meals
  • Place gifts under the tree
  • Fill stockings
  • Charge phone and camera
  • Take photos of everything on Christmas Day

Organize your photos before the new year

Here’s one more task most people skip but wish they hadn’t: go through your 2026 photos before the year ends. After Christmas, while things are quiet, is the perfect time.

The average person takes over 2,000 photos per year. By December, your camera roll is a sprawling mix of screenshots, blurry shots, duplicates, and genuine memories worth keeping. If you don’t sort through them now, you probably never will - and next year’s photos will bury this year’s.

What to do:

  • Delete the obvious junk - screenshots, accidental shots, blurry photos
  • Create albums by event: vacation, birthday parties, holidays, everyday moments
  • Back up everything to a cloud service (Google Photos, iCloud, Dropbox)
  • Turn the best photos into a photo book - a year-in-review book is one of the most rewarding ways to preserve memories, and it makes a fantastic New Year gift for your family

A year from now, you’ll be glad you did this. Photos that sit unsorted on a phone might as well not exist - nobody scrolls through thousands of images for fun. A curated album or a printed book turns those files into something you actually revisit.

The gift that needs the most lead time

Most gifts you can buy at the last minute - a gift card, a sweater, a bottle of whiskey. Those are on-the-shelf purchases.

A personalized Christmas photo book is different. It’s one of the most meaningful gifts you can give, but it needs planning because someone is designing it for you. Here’s the timeline:

Step Time needed
Share your photos, ReMeet designs the book 72 hours
You review the proof, request any changes 1-2 days
Printing and shipping after final approval 5-7 business days
Total: order to doorstep ~2 weeks

That means the safe deadline for a printed photo book delivered by Christmas is December 1. If you only need the digital version - which looks beautiful on a tablet, laptop, or TV - you can order as late as December 20 and still get the PDF in time to wrap it as a gift.

What makes a photo book a great Christmas gift? It’s personal, it’s permanent, and it gets better with time. Five years from now, nobody remembers the candle or the socks. But the book with the family vacation photos, the birthday party shots, the random Tuesday evening when everyone was laughing - that stays on the shelf and gets pulled out every holiday.
