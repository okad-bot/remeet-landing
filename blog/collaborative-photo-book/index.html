How to Make a Photo Book with Photos from Multiple People

Updated Nov 2026 · by Olga, designer at ReMeet, 10 years in editorial design

Set up a shared Google Photos or iCloud album. Send the link to everyone who has photos. Once they've added theirs, share the album with me and I'll sort by date, remove duplicates, pick the best shots, and design every page. First draft in 72 hours. From $29.

The hard part is collecting, not designing

Making a group photo book sounds simple until you try it. You need photos from 5, 10, maybe 30 people. Everyone shot on different phones. Some will text you photos. Some will email a zip file. Someone will share a Dropbox link that expires in a week. And half the group will say "I'll send them later" and never do.

A shared album fixes this. One link, one place. Everyone adds their photos. No texting, no emailing, no "can you send me that one from dinner?"

I recommend Google Photos for most group projects. It works on any phone, doesn't need an app download, and handles thousands of photos without slowing down. The link works even for people who don't have a Google account.

For iPhone-only groups, iCloud Shared Albums work well. Everyone can add photos directly from their Photos app.

Getting people to actually contribute

The biggest challenge isn't the technology. It's getting people to add their photos before you lose momentum. Here's what works:

  • Send the link with a deadline. "Add your photos by Sunday" works better than "add them when you can."
  • Send a reminder 2 days before the deadline. One message, not five.
  • Tell people how many photos to add. "Your 20-30 best photos" is better than "all your photos." Some people will dump 500 photos of the same sunset.
  • Accept that some people won't contribute. Start with what you have. You can always add more photos later.
  • Add your own photos first. An empty album is uninviting. Put in 20-30 photos so people see what it looks like.

If someone sends photos by text or email instead of using the album, just save them to the album yourself. It takes 30 seconds and avoids a back-and-forth about how to use Google Photos.

What happens when you share the album with me

Once the album has enough photos (usually 50-500 depending on the project), share the link with me. Here's what I do:

  1. Sort by date. Photos from multiple phones all get organized chronologically.
  2. Remove duplicates. When four people photograph the same toast at dinner, I pick the best version.
  3. Remove blurry and low-quality shots. AI flags these automatically and I confirm.
  4. Pick the strongest photos. For a 30-page book, I select about 50-80 photos from whatever you send.
  5. Design every page. No templates. Each spread is laid out by hand with attention to spacing, pacing, and visual rhythm.

You get a designed proof in 72 hours. Two rounds of changes are included. Tell me what to adjust in plain words.

Use cases for collaborative books

Team or nonprofit season book. Multiple coaches, parents, or volunteers contribute photos from the whole season. One designed book, copies for everyone.

Frequently asked questions

How many people can contribute to one album?

Google Photos shared albums support up to 20,000 photos and any number of contributors. iCloud Shared Albums support up to 100 contributors and 5,000 photos. For most projects, either works fine.

What if people send me photos by text or email instead?

Save them to the shared album yourself. Some people won't use the album no matter how many times you ask. That's fine. Just add their photos to the same album and I'll sort everything together.

How do you handle duplicate photos from different people?

AI identifies duplicates and near-duplicates automatically. When three people photograph the same moment, I pick the best version and discard the rest. You don't need to sort or deduplicate anything.

Can each contributor get their own printed copy?

Yes. Extra printed copies are $69 each. Order as many as you need and ship them to different addresses. US and Canada shipping is included with each copy. Europe is +$15.
