What goes in a couple photo book

Most couples have hundreds of photos spread across two phones, three cloud accounts, and a few forgotten social media archives. The question isn't whether you have enough. It's knowing which ones belong in the book.

Here's what I typically include when I design a couple book:

  • Dating photos. The early selfies, the first trip, the first holiday together. These are often low resolution and poorly lit. They're also the most valuable photos in the book.
  • Trips and adventures. Weekend getaways, road trips, that one vacation where everything went wrong and you still talk about it.
  • The apartment. Moving day, the first dinner you cooked there, the living room before and after you furnished it. Home photos feel insignificant when you take them. They become irreplaceable once you move out.
  • The proposal and engagement. If you have photos, great. If you don't, a simple text page with the date and location works just as well.
  • The wedding. Even a few favorites from the photographer can anchor a full chapter.
  • Everyday moments. Sunday mornings, cooking together, the dog between you on the couch, grocery runs, birthday dinners at home. These are the pages you'll revisit most.

Don't worry about organizing any of this yourself. Send me everything. I sort through it, pick the strongest shots, and build the layout.

Chronological vs thematic organization

There are two ways to structure a couple photo book. Both work.

Chronological order starts with the earliest photo you have together and moves forward. First date, first trip, first apartment, proposal, wedding, present day. It reads like a story with a natural arc. This approach works best for anniversary gifts because the recipient can see the full timeline in one sitting.

Thematic organization groups photos by category instead of date. All the travel photos in one section. All the home photos in another. Holidays together. Friends and family. Pets. This works well for longer relationships where a strict timeline would leave awkward gaps.

You can also combine the two. I often build a chronological backbone and let certain themes get their own spreads. A two-page spread of every New Year's Eve together. A collage of every apartment you've shared. These thematic detours give the book personality without breaking the narrative flow.

Combining photos from two phones

You both have photos the other person has never seen. Getting them into one book is simple:

  1. Create a shared Google Photos album and both add your photos to it
  2. Share the album link with me

Or send two separate album links. I merge everything and sort by timestamp, so the story flows in order regardless of which phone took the shot. Duplicates get caught automatically, and I keep the sharper version.

If one of you uses iCloud and the other uses Google Photos, that's fine. I work with both. Send whatever links you have.

Adding handwritten notes and memorabilia

A couple photo book doesn't have to be only photos. Some of the best pages I've designed include:

  • Scanned handwritten notes or letters
  • Ticket stubs from concerts, movies, or flights
  • Screenshots of text messages from important moments
  • Restaurant receipts from anniversary dinners
  • Hand-drawn maps of trips

Photograph or scan these items and include them in your album. I'll place them alongside related photos in the layout. A boarding pass next to your arrival photos. A movie ticket beside the selfie you took in the theater lobby.

Don't overdo it. Two or three pieces of memorabilia per chapter is plenty. They work best as small accents between photographs, not as the main content.
