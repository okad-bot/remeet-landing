Family Reunion Photo Book: Collect Everyone's Photos

A family reunion photo book gathers photos from every branch of the family into one designed book. Create a shared album, have everyone add their photos, and I design it in 72 hours. From $29 for the design. Printed copies for each household at $69 each.

At every reunion, twenty people take photos on twenty phones. Some get shared in the group chat, most don't. A month later, someone asks for the group photo and nobody can find it.

A reunion book solves this. One shared album collects everything from everyone. A designer organizes it into a book. Every family gets a copy. The photos end up somewhere permanent instead of buried on someone's phone.

Start the album before the reunion

The biggest mistake is waiting until after the event to collect photos. By then, people have moved on and the momentum is gone. Here's the better approach:

  1. Create a shared Google Photos album one week before. Name it clearly: "Family Reunion 2026." Send the link to every family branch.
  2. During the reunion, remind people. "Don't forget to add your photos to the shared album." A quick announcement at dinner or a text in the group chat.
  3. After the reunion, set a deadline. "Everyone add your photos by next Sunday." Send one follow-up reminder on Thursday.
  4. Share the album link with me. I sort through everything, pick the best shots, and design the book.

What makes a good reunion book

  • The big group photo. Every reunion needs at least one. If you got a few variations, I pick the one where the most people are looking at the camera.
  • Branch photos. Each family unit, each generation. Grandparents with all the grandchildren. The cousins together. The siblings.
  • Activities. Games, hikes, cooking, swimming, conversations on the porch. The candid moments that show what the reunion actually felt like.
  • Food. The table spread, someone's signature dish, grilling, the dessert table. Food photos feel like the reunion more than posed shots do.
  • Three-generation moments. A grandparent holding a baby. A parent with their own parent. These are the photos that matter most ten years later.

Getting photos from everyone

Some family members will add photos immediately. Others will forget. Here's how to handle it:

  • Tech-savvy family. The shared Google Photos link is enough. They'll add photos on their own.
  • Older relatives. Sit with them during the reunion for five minutes and help them add their photos directly. Or have them text their photos to you, and you add them to the album.
  • The person who takes the most photos. Every family has one. Make sure their photos are in the album. That's usually half the book right there.

Don't worry about getting photos from everyone. If you have 100 to 200 photos from five or six people, that's more than enough for a 30-page book.

Frequently asked questions

How do I collect photos from the whole family?

Create a shared Google Photos album before the reunion and send the link to every branch of the family. During and after the event, everyone adds their photos. When the album is complete, share it with me. I sort through everything and pick the best shots.

What if some family members are not tech-savvy?

Have them text or email their photos to one person who adds them to the shared album. Or during the reunion itself, sit down with them for five minutes and help them add photos directly. You don't need every person to contribute for the book to be complete.

How many copies can I order?

As many as you need. Each printed copy is $69 for hardcover or $129 for premium layflat. Many families order one for each household. US and Canada shipping is included, Europe is +$15.

Can I add captions with people's names?

Yes. Send a note identifying who is in the group photos. I add names as captions. This is especially useful when the book goes to cousins or extended family who may not recognize everyone. Send the names during the revision rounds or alongside the album.
