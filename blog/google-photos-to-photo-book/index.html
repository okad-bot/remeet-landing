Google Photos to Photo Book: The Easiest Way in 2026

September 2026 · 6 min read

You have 12,000 photos in Google Photos. Maybe 20,000. Birthday parties, vacations, random Tuesday afternoons — all backed up automatically, all sitting there unseen.

You've thought about making a photo book. But then you open a photo book editor, and it asks you to choose photos one by one, drag them into layouts, and design each page. You close the tab. Maybe next weekend.

That weekend never comes.

Here's the good news: in 2026, there are easier ways to turn your Google Photos into a real book. Some require a little effort. One requires almost none.

Option 1: Google Photos' built-in photo book tool

Google Photos has a built-in "Print store" feature that lets you create photo books directly from your library.

How it works:

  1. Open Google Photos on the web
  2. Go to Print store → Photo books
  3. Select an album or pick photos manually
  4. Google auto-generates a layout
  5. Rearrange and edit as needed
  6. Order (softcover from ~$15, hardcover from ~$30)

Pros:

  • Built right into Google Photos — no new account needed
  • Affordable pricing
  • Auto-layout saves some time

Cons:

  • Design quality is basic — simple grids, minimal typography
  • Limited to 140 photos per book
  • No curation — you still pick every photo yourself
  • No chapters, no captions, no editorial design
  • The result looks like what it is: an auto-generated layout

Best for: Quick, cheap books when you've already selected your favorite photos and don't mind a simple design.

Option 2: Export to a photo book service

Services like Mixbook, Artifact Uprising, and Shutterfly let you upload photos from your phone or computer. The workflow looks like this:

  1. Select photos in Google Photos
  2. Download them to your device
  3. Upload to the photo book service
  4. Design your book using their editor
  5. Order

The problem:

This process has two friction points. First, downloading and re-uploading hundreds of photos is tedious. Second, you're back to designing the book yourself — choosing layouts, placing photos, writing text. For a 60-page book, expect 2-4 hours of work.

Best for: People who enjoy the design process and want more control over the final result.

Option 3: Share your Google Photos and let someone else do it

This is the approach that didn't exist a few years ago. Instead of downloading, uploading, and designing — you just share a link to your Google Photos album. A team of designers goes through your photos, curates the best ones, and creates a complete book for you.

How it works with Remeet:

  1. Open Google Photos
  2. Select the photos or albums you want in your book (or just share everything from a time period)
  3. Create a shared link
  4. Paste the link into Remeet's order form
  5. Add any notes ("focus on our trip to Portugal" or "this is for our baby's first year")
  6. Get a designed proof in 72 hours

  8. Approve and order your printed book

Total time spent: about 5 minutes.

What happens behind the scenes:

When you share 2,000 photos, we don't just randomly pick 100. Here's the actual process:

  • Duplicate removal — Those 14 nearly-identical shots of the same moment get narrowed down to the strongest one
  • Quality filtering — Blurry photos, screenshots, and accidental shots are removed
  • Story building — Photos are grouped into chapters based on events, locations, and time periods
  • Editorial design — Each spread is composed with intentional layout, typography, and pacing
  • Captions — Contextual captions are added based on photo metadata and your notes

Best for: People with large photo libraries who want a beautiful result without spending hours on design.

Tips for sharing your Google Photos

Whichever service you choose, here are some tips to get the best results:

  • Don't over-curate before sharing. It's tempting to pre-select your favorites, but professional designers often find gems you'd overlook. When in doubt, share more, not less.
  • Use albums or date ranges. Instead of selecting photos one by one, share an existing album or create a quick one filtered by date. "All photos from June 2025 to June 2026" is perfectly fine.
  • Add context in notes. "We moved to a new house in March" or "The birthday party was at grandma's" helps designers tell a better story.
  • Include videos if you can. Some services can pull still frames from your best video moments — scenes you might not have captured as photos.

The real barrier isn't the tool

The reason most photo books never get made isn't that the tools are too expensive or too complicated. It's that the project feels overwhelming. Three thousand photos is a lot to sort through. Designing 60 pages is a lot of work.

The best approach is the one that actually gets done. If you enjoy designing, use Mixbook or Artifact Uprising. If you want it cheap and fast, use Google's built-in tool. If you want it beautiful and don't want to do the work, share your photos with Remeet.
