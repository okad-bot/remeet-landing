How Many Photos Do You Need for a Photo Book?

For a 30-page ReMeet book, 50 to 200 photos is a good starting range. For 100 pages, 200 to 500 works well. You can send up to 5,000 photos and I pick the best ones. More photos means more to choose from, not a bigger book. Designed from $29, proof in 72 hours.

These are guidelines, not rules. I have designed 30-page books from 30 photos and from 2,000. The book size stays the same. What changes is how much I have to choose from.

What happens when you send too many photos

Nothing bad. This is actually the best scenario.

When you send 500 photos for a 30-page book, the software sorts them first. It groups photos by date and location, removes exact duplicates, flags blurry frames, and picks the sharpest version of burst shots. That usually cuts the pile in half.

Then I go through what's left. I look for variety: different moments, different people, a mix of wide shots and close-ups. I pick the photos that tell the story and work well together on a spread. The rest stay in your album. Nothing gets deleted.

If you're not sure about a batch, send them all. It's faster for me to skip a photo than it is for you to decide whether to include it.

What happens when you send very few photos

If you send 30 to 50 photos for a 30-page book, every image matters more. I use most of them, give key shots a full page or even a double spread, and add more white space between images. The result feels more like a gallery than a scrapbook.

This works especially well for professional photoshoots, milestone events, or any situation where every photo was already curated by a photographer.

Below about 20 photos, a 30-page book starts to feel sparse. I would suggest a shorter book or pairing the photos with handwritten notes, quotes, or captions to fill the space naturally.

How the selection actually works

Here's what happens after you share your album link:

  1. Software pass. The AI scans every photo. It groups images by date, removes duplicates, flags blurry and dark frames, and picks the sharpest version of burst sequences. It never changes or generates photos.
  2. Designer review. I go through the sorted set and pick the photos for the book. I look for variety, emotional moments, and images that pair well on a spread.
  3. Layout. I design every page by hand. Some photos get a full page, some share a spread. The layout depends on the content, not a template.
  4. Your review. You see the proof in 72 hours. Want to swap a photo? Add one I skipped? Just tell me the page and I'll update it. Two revision rounds included.

Frequently asked questions

What if I only have 30 photos?

30 photos is enough for a short, focused book. I design a 30-page book with 1 to 2 photos per spread and generous white space. Fewer photos often means a more editorial, gallery-like feel. If you have fewer than 20, consider adding handwritten notes or captions to fill out the pages naturally.

Can I send 5,000 photos?

Yes. 5,000 is the maximum per book. The software sorts them by date, flags duplicates and blurry shots, and ranks the rest by quality. I review the top picks and design the book from there. You do not need to sort or select anything beforehand.

Do all my photos get used?

No. If you send 500 photos for a 30-page book, roughly 60 to 90 make it into the final layout. The rest stay in your album, untouched. I pick the ones that tell the story best and work well together on a page.

Can I choose which photos go in?

Yes. After you see the first draft, you can swap any photo. Tell me which ones to add or remove, and I update the layout in the next revision round. Two revision rounds are included in the price.

What counts as one photo?

Every image file counts as one photo. Screenshots, scanned notes, and kid artwork all count. Live Photos count as one still image. Videos are separate and require the video extraction add-on (+$50).
