How to Choose Photos for a Photo Book

Why choosing photos is so hard

Most people get stuck at the selection step. You have 2,000 photos from a vacation or a year with a baby. You open the album, start scrolling, and realize you can't decide what to keep and what to cut. Everything feels important. Or nothing feels important enough.

An hour later, you've selected 12 photos and given up. The book never gets made.

Here's the thing: selecting photos is a design skill. It's not about picking your favorites. It's about picking photos that work together on a page. That's why most people find it easier to send everything and let someone else choose. But if you want to do it yourself, here's how I think about it after 10 years of designing books.

The variety rule

A good photo book has visual variety on every spread. That means mixing different types of shots:

Shot typeWhat it doesExample
Wide shotSets the sceneThe beach, the venue, the nursery
Medium shotShows the peopleTwo people at a table, a group photo
Close-upShows emotion or detailA face, hands, a reaction
Detail shotAdds textureFood, flowers, a ticket, a sign
CandidCaptures the real momentLaughing, dancing, not looking at the camera

If you pick five photos of people smiling at the camera, every page looks the same. If you mix a landscape, a candid, a detail, and a portrait, every spread feels different. That's what makes a book interesting to flip through.

What to cut first

Start by removing these:

  • Near-duplicates - you took 8 photos of the same moment, keep the best 1-2
  • Completely blurry shots - if you can't tell what's in the photo, skip it
  • Bad lighting with no redeeming quality - too dark to see, too bright to make out faces
  • Accidental photos - pocket shots, blurry floor, screen recordings

That usually cuts your collection by 30-50% right away. The rest is about choosing which moments to include, not which photos are technically good enough.

Keep the imperfect ones

The best photos in a book are often the ones you'd almost delete. The blurry action shot where someone is mid-laugh. The dimly lit bedtime routine. The screenshot of the text message where someone said "it's a boy." The hotel room selfie at the end of a long day.

These photos have feeling. They tell the story between the highlights. A book of only perfect, well-lit, smiling photos feels like a magazine ad. A book with messy, real, in-between moments feels like life.

When in doubt, include it. It's easier to remove a photo from a layout than to wish you'd kept it.

How many photos for each book size

A rough guide for how many photos to aim for:

  • 30-page book - 60 to 120 photos (2-4 per page, plus some full-bleed spreads)
  • 100-page book - 200 to 400 photos (same ratio, more room for the story)

These are guidelines, not rules. Some pages look best with one large photo. Others work with four or five smaller ones. For a detailed breakdown, see how many photos for a 30 or 100 page book.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos should I select for a photo book?

For a 30-page book, 60-120 photos is a good range. For 100 pages, 200-400 works well. But if you use ReMeet, you can send up to 5,000 photos without selecting anything. The designer picks the best ones for the layout.

Should I include blurry or dark photos?

Skip anything that's genuinely unreadable. But a slightly soft photo with a great expression beats a sharp photo with a stiff pose. Don't be too strict with technical quality. The moment matters more.

What if I have too many photos of the same thing?

Pick the one or two strongest versions and skip the rest. If you took 15 photos of the same sunset, the best one is enough. Near-duplicates are the first thing to cut when narrowing down.

Can I just send all my photos without selecting?

Yes. With ReMeet, you share your full album link and the designer picks the best shots. AI removes duplicates and blurry frames, then the designer selects and arranges the photos that work best for the layout. No pre-selection needed.

Should I include screenshots and non-photo images?

Yes, if they tell part of the story. Flight confirmations, text message announcements, pregnancy test screenshots, the weather app on a perfect day. These make great small accent images in a book layout.
