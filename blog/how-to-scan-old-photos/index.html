How to Scan Old Photos for a Photo Book

You can scan old printed photos with your phone camera or a flatbed scanner, then add them to a Google Photos album alongside your digital photos. I accept any resolution and design around it, placing lower-res scans at the right size. First proof in 72 hours, from $29.

You have boxes of printed photos from the 1980s and 1990s. Some are in albums, some are loose in a shoebox, some are framed. You want them in a photo book, but they're not digital. The good news: scanning them is easier than you think. Your phone is enough.

Three ways to scan old photos

1. Phone camera (fastest)

This is the simplest option and it works for most photo books. Here's how:

  1. Place the photo on a flat, well-lit surface. Near a window during the day works best.
  2. Hold your phone directly above the photo, parallel to the surface. Avoid tilting.
  3. Make sure your shadow isn't covering the photo.
  4. Tap to focus, then take the photo.
  5. Crop out the background (table, floor) in your phone's photo editor.

For glossy prints that catch the light, angle the photo slightly or move your light source. The goal is no glare on the surface.

2. Google PhotoScan app (best for glossy prints)

Google PhotoScan is a free app for iOS and Android. It takes multiple exposures from different angles and combines them to remove glare. This works much better than the regular camera on glossy or laminated prints.

  1. Open PhotoScan and point it at the printed photo.
  2. The app shows four dots. Move your phone over each dot to capture the angles.
  3. The app stitches the captures together and removes glare automatically.
  4. The result saves to your camera roll.

It takes about 10 seconds per photo. For a box of 100 prints, plan about 20 minutes.

3. Flatbed scanner (best quality)

If you have a flatbed scanner or an all-in-one printer with a scanner, this gives the highest resolution. Set it to 300-600 DPI and save as JPEG. At 600 DPI, a 4x6 print becomes a 2400x3600 pixel file, enough for a full-page spread in any book.

Flatbed scanning is slower. Each photo takes 30 to 60 seconds. Use this method when you have a small number of important photos you want at the highest quality, like a wedding portrait or a family formal.

Tips for better scans

  • Clean the photos first. Wipe dust off with a soft cloth. Dust shows up as spots in the scan.
  • Don't use flash. It creates hotspots on glossy prints. Use natural daylight or a desk lamp off to the side.
  • Scan in batches. Do ten photos at a time, then upload to Google Photos. It's less overwhelming than trying to do 200 in one sitting.
  • Don't crop too tight. Leave a small border around the photo. I crop precisely during design.
  • Adhesive album pages. If photos are glued to sticky pages, photograph the whole page. I crop individual photos from the page scan. Don't peel photos off because it can tear them.

What to do with the scans

  1. Add all scans to a Google Photos album alongside your digital photos.
  2. If you know the dates, add them to the file names or album description: "1985-christmas.jpg" or "1990s-vacation."
  3. Share the album link with me. I organize everything chronologically and design the book.

Frequently asked questions

Is a phone scan good enough for printing?

Yes, for most photo book pages. Modern phones capture enough detail for a 4x6 or 5x7 placement on a page. For a full-bleed double-page spread, a flatbed scanner at 600 DPI gives better results, but a phone scan still works at a smaller size.

What app should I use to scan photos?

Google PhotoScan is free and removes glare from glossy prints. The regular camera app works too if you avoid glare and shadows. For large batches, apps like Pic Scanner or Photomyne scan multiple photos per frame.

How should I organize scanned photos?

If you know the approximate dates, rename files with the year or decade (1985-christmas.jpg). If not, group by era or event in separate album folders. I organize them into the final layout regardless, but dates help with chronological books.

Can I mix scanned prints with digital photos?

Yes. Add both scanned prints and digital photos to the same Google Photos album. I handle the different resolutions and place each image at the right size on the page. Mixing eras is what makes milestone books interesting.

What about photos stuck in old albums with adhesive pages?

If the photos are glued down, photograph the album pages as they are. I can crop individual photos from the page scan. Don't force photos off adhesive pages because that can damage them permanently.
