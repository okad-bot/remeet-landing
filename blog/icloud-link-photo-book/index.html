iCloud Link vs Shared Album for a Photo Book

If your photo book prints look soft or blurry, you probably shared an iCloud Shared Album instead of an iCloud link. Shared Albums compress every photo to 2048 pixels. You spent an hour selecting your 200 best photos. You shared them with a photo book service. The proof arrived and everything looked slightly blurry. Not terrible, but not sharp either.

It wasn't the printer. It wasn't your camera. It was how you shared the photos.

Apple gives you two ways to share photos from your iPhone: an iCloud link and a Shared Album. They look similar, but one compresses your images and the other does not.

The key number is 2048 pixels. A Shared Album resizes every photo so the longest edge is at most 2048px. That is about 1.7 megapixels. Your iPhone 15 shoots at 48 MP. Your iPhone 12 shoots at 12 MP. Either way, you're losing a lot.

For a full-page spread in a hardcover book, the photo needs at least 3,000 pixels on the long side to look sharp at 300 DPI. A Shared Album photo can't reach that. The result: visible softness, especially on close-ups and text in photos.

How to create an iCloud link (step by step)

It takes about two minutes.

On iPhone or iPad:

  1. Open the Photos app
  2. Select the photos you want to share (tap Select, then tap each photo, or select by date)
  3. Tap the Share button (square with arrow)
  4. Scroll down and tap Copy iCloud Link
  5. Wait a few seconds while the link is prepared
  6. Paste the link wherever you need it

On Mac:

  1. Open Photos
  2. Select the photos (click the first, Shift-click the last, or Cmd-click to pick individual ones)
  3. Right-click and choose Share → Copy iCloud Link
  4. Paste the link

The link is valid for 30 days. If your photo book project takes longer, you can create a new link. I'll let you know if the link expires while I'm working on your book.

Why Shared Albums compress your photos

Apple designed Shared Albums for casual sharing, not printing. When you invite someone to a Shared Album, they get a lightweight copy that loads quickly on any device and doesn't eat into anyone's storage. Good for showing vacation photos to family. Bad for printing.

The compression is permanent within the Shared Album. Even if you download a photo from a Shared Album you were invited to, you get the compressed version, not the original. The only way to get the full file is from the original owner's library, shared via an iCloud link or AirDrop.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send a Shared Album for my photo book anyway?

You can, but expect softer prints. Shared Albums cap photos at 2048px on the long edge. That is enough for a small softcover but not ideal for a large hardcover where photos fill the whole page. An iCloud link gives me the original files, which always print sharper.

What if I have photos on both iPhone and Android?

Send both. Share an iCloud link for your iPhone photos and a Google Photos album link for the Android photos. I combine them into one book, sorted by date. You can also upload everything to one Google Photos album and share that single link.

How many photos can I share via an iCloud link?

An iCloud link can hold up to 5,000 photos. If you have more, split them into two links or use Google Photos, which handles larger libraries. For a 30-page book, 100 to 300 photos is a good starting point. For 100 pages, 300 to 1,000 works well.

Does ReMeet keep my photos after the book is done?

No. I download your photos only to design the book. Once you approve the final version and your printed copy ships, the working files are deleted. Your iCloud link stays under your control and you can revoke it anytime.
