Kids Artwork Photo Book: Turn Drawings into a Printed Book

By Olga, designer at ReMeet, 10 years in editorial design

Children's drawings pile up fast. Photograph or scan the artwork, send the album to ReMeet, and I'll design a printed book that preserves every piece in order. One book replaces bins of crumpled paper. It makes a perfect end-of-year gift for grandparents or a keepsake your child will treasure as an adult. Design starts at $29.

Why the pile never gets organized

Kids bring home drawings, paintings, and crafts at a steady pace. You stick the best ones on the fridge, toss a few into a drawer, and promise yourself you'll sort through them later.

Later doesn't come. The drawer fills up. The paint flakes. After a year or two, it's hard to tell what was drawn at age four versus age six. You feel guilty throwing anything away, but you can't store every finger painting forever.

A photo book solves this. Photograph the artwork once, and I arrange it into a book you can actually open and enjoy. The paper originals can go. The art stays.

How to photograph artwork at home

You don't need a scanner or special equipment. A phone camera does the job. Here's how to get clean results:

  • Lay the art flat on a table. Don't hold it up against a wall where it curls or casts shadows.
  • Use natural light. Near a window, no flash. Flash creates glare on shiny paint, crayon, and glitter.
  • Shoot from directly above. Hold your phone parallel to the paper so edges don't warp.
  • Don't crop too tight. Leave a small border around the artwork. I'll clean up the framing during design.
  • Include the name and date if visible. If your child signed the drawing or the teacher wrote the date, make sure it shows in the photo.
  • Batch by year or age. If you're photographing a backlog, sort the art into rough piles by school year before you start shooting. It saves time later.

For 3D crafts like clay figures or popsicle stick creations, photograph them on a clean white background from two or three angles. I can arrange multiple angles on one spread.

If you already have artwork in Google Photos, just share the album link. No need to download anything.

What goes in the book

You don't have to include every single piece. I recommend 40 to 60 pieces for a 30-page book, or up to 150 for a larger 100-page collection that spans multiple years.

Here's what works well:

  • First recognizable person (the classic circle-head, stick-body family portrait)
  • First time they wrote their name
  • Holiday and birthday cards they made by hand
  • Paintings where the colors are genuinely striking
  • Funny drawings with stories behind them ("this is our dog wearing a spaceship")
  • School projects that show real effort
  • Anything with a handprint or footprint

Skip duplicates. If there are twelve nearly identical rainbows, pick the best two. It's also fine to include a photo of your child making the art. Those candid shots give the book warmth.

If you're unsure how many photos to include, I can help you pick once you share your album.

How I design the book

Once you send your album, I organize the artwork chronologically. Each section gets an age or school year label. The strongest pieces get a full page. Smaller drawings go into grids of four or six, grouped by color palette or theme.

I use white or cream backgrounds so the artwork's colors show clearly. If you've included captions (the child's age, what they said the drawing was), I add those in a clean typeface below each image.

The result looks intentional. Not a scrapbook. A proper book that shows your child's creative growth from early scribbles to real drawings.

You get the first draft as a PDF within 72 hours. Two rounds of revisions are included. Nothing prints until you approve.

A gift grandparents love

This is one of my most popular gift orders. Parents make the book for themselves, then order extra copies for grandparents. It's a gift that actually gets opened and displayed, not tucked away in a closet.

End-of-school-year timing works well. Collect artwork from September to June, photograph it over a weekend, and have the book ready by summer. Kids who attend daycare or preschool produce especially colorful collections because every craft project is different.

You can also make one book per year and build a series. By the time your child is a teenager, you'll have a shelf showing every stage.

Frequently asked questions

How many pieces of artwork should I include?

A 30-page book fits roughly 40 to 60 pieces, depending on layout. Some pages show one large painting, others hold a grid of four or six smaller drawings. If your child has a year's worth of art, pick the best 50 and you'll have a full book.

Do I need a scanner or is a phone photo enough?

A phone photo is enough for most artwork. Lay the piece flat on a table, use natural light from a window, and shoot from directly above. No flash. The result prints clearly at full page size. A scanner gives slightly sharper detail, but it's not required.

Can I include 3D crafts like clay figures or paper mache?

Yes. Photograph them on a clean background from two or three angles. A white sheet of paper behind the object works well. I can arrange multiple angles on a single spread so you see the full piece.

What if my child's artwork spans several years?

That's the best kind of book. I organize the artwork chronologically so you can see the progression from early scribbles to detailed drawings. Each section gets a year or age label. A 100-page book ($79) works well for multi-year collections.

Is this a good gift for grandparents?

It's one of the most popular gift orders I get. Grandparents love it because they see every drawing but don't have to store the paper. Extra printed copies are $69 each with US and Canada shipping included.
