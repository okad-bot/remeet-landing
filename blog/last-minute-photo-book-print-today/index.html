Last-Minute Photo Book You Can Print at a Copy Shop

November 2026 · 9 min read
Written by Olga Miller, founder of ReMeet

You need a photo book. Maybe it is a birthday tomorrow, an anniversary this weekend, or you just realized the holidays are closer than you thought. Whatever the reason, the question is the same: how fast can you get a photo book printed?

Short answer: If you need a physical book in your hands today, Walgreens and CVS offer same-day pickup. You will spend 2-3 hours in their editor doing all the layout work yourself, and the quality is basic. If you can wait 72 hours for a designed digital proof plus a few days for shipping, ReMeet handles everything for you. Total effort on your part: about 5 minutes.

Below is a complete breakdown of every option, from same-day to one-week turnaround, so you can pick the right path for your timeline.

Same-day pickup options

These are your only choices if you need a printed photo book within hours. All of them are fully DIY: you upload the photos, pick a template, place every image, and adjust the layout yourself.

Walgreens Photo

Walgreens is the most widely available same-day photo book option in the United States. You build the book on their website or app, then pick it up at a nearby store, usually within a few hours.

  • Formats: Softcover and window cover books in a few sizes (4x6, 5x7, 8.5x11)
  • Starting price: Around $10 for a small softcover (frequently on sale for less)
  • Editor: Browser-based drag-and-drop. Limited templates. No auto-layout.
  • Print quality: Acceptable for a quick gift. Not comparable to professional photo book printers.
  • Your time: 2-3 hours minimum for a 20-page book

Walgreens runs frequent coupons (40-60% off), so check for a promo code before you start. The editor works but feels dated. Expect to spend time resizing and repositioning photos that do not fit the template slots cleanly.

CVS Photo

CVS offers a similar same-day photo book service. The process is nearly identical to Walgreens: upload photos, build the book online, pick up in store.

  • Formats: Softcover books, limited sizes
  • Starting price: Around $13 for a basic book
  • Editor: Comparable to Walgreens. Basic templates, manual placement.
  • Print quality: Similar to Walgreens. Fine for casual use.
  • Your time: 2-3 hours

CVS has fewer locations than Walgreens in many areas, so check availability at your nearest store before you commit to building the book.

Walmart Photo

Walmart offers same-day pickup for individual photo prints and some photo products, but their photo book same-day availability varies by location. Some stores support it; many do not. If you have a Walmart nearby, check their website first to confirm same-day book pickup is available at that specific store.

  • Starting price: Around $8 for a small softcover
  • Editor: Basic. Similar limitations to Walgreens.
  • Print quality: Basic consumer grade
  • Your time: 2-3 hours, plus a trip to the store

What all same-day options have in common: You do all the work. You pick the photos, arrange every page, crop and resize images to fit template slots, add text if you want it, and review the whole book before ordering. For a 20-page book, plan on at least 2 hours. For a longer book, expect 3-4 hours or more.

Fast online options (1-3 day shipping)

If you do not need the book today but want it within a few days, several online photo book services offer rush or expedited shipping. The book quality is better than drugstore prints, but you still design everything yourself.

Shutterfly

Shutterfly is one of the largest online photo book services. They offer rush processing (1 business day) and expedited shipping options that can get a book to you in 2-3 business days total.

  • Rush processing: $14.99 extra for 1-business-day production
  • Expedited shipping: $14.99-$24.99 depending on speed
  • Book prices: Start around $10 for a softcover (heavy discounts are common, 40-50% off)
  • Editor: More polished than drugstore options. Auto-fill feature places photos for you, but you will almost certainly want to rearrange.
  • Your time: 2-4 hours for a decent result

The total cost adds up quickly. A 30-page hardcover with rush processing and fast shipping can run $50-70 after discounts, and you still spend the afternoon in the editor. For a deeper comparison of options, see our best photo book services guide.

Mixbook

Mixbook produces higher-quality books than Shutterfly, with better paper and more flexible layout tools. They offer expedited production and shipping.

  • Rush production: Available for an extra fee
  • Expedited shipping: 2-day and overnight options
  • Book prices: Start around $20 for a softcover, $35+ for hardcover
  • Editor: Well-designed. More layout flexibility than Shutterfly.
  • Your time: 3-5 hours for a book you are happy with

Mixbook is a good choice if quality matters and you enjoy the design process. If you do not enjoy the design process, 3-5 hours in any editor is a long time. See our price comparison for a detailed cost breakdown across services.

Other online options

Snapfish, Amazon Prints, and Google Photos (via CVS or Walmart) all offer photo books with various shipping speeds. The pattern is the same: you build the book, pay extra for faster shipping, and hope it arrives on time. Production typically takes 1-3 business days before shipping even begins.

When each option makes sense

Same-day pickup (Walgreens, CVS, Walmart) makes sense when you absolutely need a physical book in hand today. Maybe you forgot a birthday and the party is tonight. Or you want to bring something to a family dinner in a few hours. The quality will not win any design awards, but it is a real, printed book with real photos, and that counts for something. Just be prepared to spend the morning in their editor.

Rush online (Shutterfly, Mixbook, Snapfish) makes sense when you have 3-5 days and want better print quality than a drugstore. If you enjoy designing things, or if you have very specific ideas about layout and page order, a DIY editor gives you full control. Plan for a full afternoon of work, plus the rush fees.

ReMeet makes sense when you value your time, want a professionally designed result, and can wait about a week and a half total (72 hours for the proof, plus shipping). It is the right choice for anyone who has hundreds of photos they have never organized, who does not enjoy drag-and-drop editors, or who simply has better things to do with 3 hours on a Saturday. It is also the only option on this list where a human designer curates and arranges your photos.

A note about "fast" vs. "easy": Same-day pickup is the fastest way to get a physical book, but it is not easy. You do all the work. ReMeet is the easiest way to get a photo book, but it is not same-day. There is no option that is both same-day and zero-effort. Pick the tradeoff that fits your situation.

Tips for a faster result

Whichever route you choose, these tips will save you time:

  1. Gather photos before you open any editor. The biggest time sink in any photo book project is scrolling through your camera roll. Create a folder or album with just the photos you want before you start building.
  2. Do not aim for perfection. A finished book is better than a perfect book that never gets made. If you are using a DIY editor, pick "good enough" layouts and move on.
  3. Use auto-fill features. Shutterfly and Mixbook both have auto-fill tools that place your photos into templates automatically. The result will not be perfect, but it gives you a starting point to edit rather than starting from blank pages.
  4. Keep it short. A 20-page book takes half the time of a 40-page book. If speed is your priority, fewer pages means faster done.
  5. Or skip the editor entirely. If you have more than a day, share your photos with ReMeet and let someone else handle the design. Your 5 minutes of effort buys you a result that would take 3-5 hours to match yourself.

What about digital-only options?

If you do not strictly need a printed book, a digital photo book can be ready much faster. With ReMeet, the digital proof is delivered as a PDF within 72 hours (weekday orders placed before noon, without edits, can be faster). You can share it by email or show it on a tablet immediately. If you decide to print later, the design is already done.

This is a good fallback for last-minute gifts. Take the PDF to a copy shop (FedEx, Staples, or a local print center) and get a bound copy the same day. Or print a single page of the proof, put it in a card, and tell the recipient the full printed book is on its way. The gesture lands the same day. The book arrives the following week.
