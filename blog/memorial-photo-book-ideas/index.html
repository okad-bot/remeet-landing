How to Make a Memorial Photo Book Without the Overwhelm

Updated Nov 2026 · 7 min read
Olga, designer at ReMeet, 10 years in editorial design

Making a memorial book is emotional, and sorting through hundreds of photos can feel impossible. Send me everything you have. Tell me the person's story in a few sentences, and I organize the photos into a book that feels like them. First draft in 72 hours, from $29.

Why memorial books are different

Most photo book projects start with excitement. You just got back from a trip, or the baby turned one, and you want to capture it. A memorial book starts differently. Someone is gone, and you're looking at their photos through a kind of weight that makes every decision harder.

Which photos matter? Should you go chronological? What about the photos where they look sick? What about the ones where they look young and happy and it hurts to see?

There's also the practical side. The photos are scattered. Some are on your phone, some are on a sibling's phone, some are printed and sitting in a shoebox at your parents' house. Nobody has a complete collection, and nobody has the energy to assemble one.

I've designed memorial books for families in exactly this situation. Here's what I've learned: the book doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to exist. A finished book that the family can hold and pass around at gatherings does more than a theoretical perfect project that never gets made.

What to include

You don't need to decide upfront. Send me everything you have, and I'll work with what's there. That said, here's what tends to make a memorial book feel complete:

  • Childhood and youth. Even a few scanned photos from early decades give the book a sense of a full life, not just the recent years.
  • Milestones. Weddings, graduations, births, retirements. The moments the family already gathers around.
  • Everyday moments. Cooking in the kitchen, reading on the porch, playing with the dog. These are often the photos people love most in the finished book.
  • Group photos. Family reunions, holiday dinners, vacations. These show the person in the middle of the people they loved.
  • Handwritten notes. Birthday cards, letters, recipes in their handwriting. Scan or photograph them and include them in your album. I place these next to the photos they belong with.
  • Their things. A photo of their garden, their workshop, their favorite chair. Objects carry memory too.

Don't worry about duplicates or quality. I remove blurry shots and near-identical photos during the design process. Send 50 photos or 2,000. More is better because it gives me more to choose from.

How to collect photos from family

This is usually the hardest part, and it doesn't have to be complicated. Here's what works:

  1. Create a shared Google Photos album. Name it something clear, like "Photos for Dad's Book." Share the link with everyone who might have photos.
  2. Give a deadline. People will add photos for months if you let them. A week is usually enough. "Add what you have by Sunday" gets results.
  3. Accept everything. Don't ask people to pick their best photos. That adds a decision they don't want to make right now. Tell them to dump everything in. I'll sort through it.
  4. Check in once. Some family members will forget. One reminder is enough. If they don't add photos, work with what you have.

If some photos are only available as prints, photograph them with your phone. A phone photo of a printed photo is good enough for the book. I adjust the layout so these images are sized and placed where they look right.

Once the shared album has everything, share the link with me. That's the last step you need to handle. I take it from there.

Letting someone else handle the design

The whole point of a done-for-you service is that you don't have to make 200 layout decisions while you're grieving. I sort the photos chronologically, pick the strongest images, remove duplicates, and design every page.

When you place your order, I'll ask you to tell me about the person in a few sentences. Not an essay. Something like: "My dad, Ray. Grew up in Ohio, worked in construction, loved fishing and his grandkids. He was funny and never sat still." That's enough for me to understand the tone the book should have.

You get a proof in 72 hours. If you want changes, tell me in plain words: "Move the fishing trip to after the birthday party," or "Can you add more white space on page 12?" Two rounds of revisions are included. Nothing prints until you say it's ready.

If you don't love the first draft, you get a full refund. No questions.

Frequently asked questions

What if the photos are old and low quality?

Old photos work fine. Scanned prints, photos of photos, and low-resolution images all go into the book. I adjust the layout so smaller or lower-quality images are placed at a size where they still look good. A slightly grainy photo from 1985 adds character, it doesn't ruin the page.

Can I include scanned letters or handwritten notes?

Yes. Scan or photograph handwritten notes, letters, cards, or even drawings. I place them next to the matching photos. A birthday card next to the party photos, a letter next to a portrait. These details make the book feel personal in a way that photos alone can't.

How many copies should I order for the family?

One per household is the usual approach. Extra printed copies are $69 each with US and Canada shipping included, so a family of four households would be $276 total for the copies plus the original design fee. You can always order more copies later.

Can multiple family members contribute photos?

Yes. The easiest way is to create a shared Google Photos album and have everyone add their photos there. Then share the album link with me. I sort through everything and pick the best shots from all contributors.
