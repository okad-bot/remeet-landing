Mixbook Alternatives: 5 Photo Book Services Compared

ReMeet is a done-for-you alternative to Mixbook. Send a link to your photos and a designer builds the book from $79 for up to 100 pages: first proof in 72 hours, two rounds of edits, printed hardcover.

November 2026 · 9 min read
Written by Olga Miller, founder of ReMeet

Mixbook is one of the better photo book editors out there. The templates are modern, the drag-and-drop editor is intuitive, and print quality is solid. Tom's Guide named it the "best overall" photo book maker in 2026.

So why are people looking for alternatives?

Usually one of three reasons. First, the design process takes a long time - even with good templates, building an 80-page book takes most people two to four hours. Second, pricing without a sale is steep - a hardcover can run $80-130. Third, some people want a book that doesn't look like it came from a template at all.

We compared five Mixbook alternatives to help you find the right fit. Each one takes a different approach to the same problem: turning your photos into a book.

What makes Mixbook good (and where it falls short)

Before comparing alternatives, it helps to be specific about what Mixbook does well and where the friction is.

Strengths:

  • Modern, customizable templates with real design sensibility
  • Flexible editor - you can adjust layouts, backgrounds, and text freely
  • Good print quality on premium paper options
  • AI Story Mode for auto-generated layouts (new in 2026)
  • Frequent 40-50% sales bring prices down significantly

Where people get stuck:

  • Too many design options can feel overwhelming rather than helpful
  • Building a book still takes hours even with templates
  • Full price ($80-130 for a hardcover) is expensive without a sale
  • Results can look template-based despite the effort
  • No help with photo curation - you still need to sort and select photos yourself

If your main frustration is price, you're looking for a cheaper alternative. If it's the time investment, you're looking for something faster. And if it's the design quality, you want something either more premium or more hands-off.

1. Artifact Uprising - the premium alternative

If you're leaving Mixbook because the results don't feel special enough, Artifact Uprising is the step up. The paper quality is noticeably better. The materials feel luxurious - linen covers, thick pages, archival-grade printing. This is the service that interior designers order from.

Pricing: 80-page hardcover runs $150-220. No frequent sales. You pay full price, and the quality justifies it.

The tradeoff: You're the designer. The editor is powerful but complex. Expect four to six hours for a good 80-page book. Most people who start an Artifact Uprising project take weeks to finish it, not because the editor is bad, but because the task is genuinely time-consuming.

Best for: People who enjoy the creative process and want museum-quality results. If you find designing relaxing rather than tedious, Artifact Uprising is hard to beat.

2. Shutterfly - the budget workhorse

Shutterfly has been around forever and still moves more photo books than almost anyone. The main appeal is price: during their frequent 40-50% sales, an 80-page hardcover can drop to $25-45. They also sell cards, mugs, blankets, and basically anything you can put a photo on.

Pricing: Softcover from ~$10, 80-page hardcover $50-90 at list price, often 40-50% off during sales.

The tradeoff: Templates feel dated compared to Mixbook. The editor can be clunky. Design options exist but they're limited. The result is a perfectly fine photo book that doesn't stand out visually.

Best for: Budget-first buyers who want a physical book at the lowest possible price and are comfortable with a template editor. Also useful if you want other photo products (cards, calendars, gifts) from the same service.

For a deeper price comparison, see our cheapest photo book services breakdown. See also: ReMeet vs Shutterfly

3. Chatbooks - the fast, small alternative

Chatbooks takes the opposite approach from Mixbook. Instead of giving you a powerful editor and hundreds of templates, it connects to your camera roll or Instagram and auto-generates a small book in minutes. The subscription model ($10-15/month) automatically prints a softcover book every time you hit a certain photo count.

Pricing: Softcover $10-15, hardcover $30-40. Subscription model available.

The tradeoff: Design is minimal - photos go in chronological order with basic borders. No editorial layout, no chapters, no curation. Books tend to feel like printed phone galleries rather than designed keepsakes. Format is small (typically 6×6").

Best for: Parents who want a quick, ongoing photo record without spending time on design. Great for monthly snapshots, less great for milestone books like a first year or a wedding.

See also: ReMeet vs Chatbooks

4. Snapfish - the sale-price alternative

Snapfish competes directly with Shutterfly on price. Their sales are even more aggressive - 50%+ off is common, and they regularly offer deals on first orders. If you're timing your purchase around a sale, Snapfish often comes out cheaper than Shutterfly.

Pricing: Softcover from ~$8, 80-page hardcover $40-70 at list price, frequently 50%+ off.

The tradeoff: Design tools are basic. Templates are limited. Print quality is acceptable but not premium - you get what you pay for. The editor can feel slow and the user experience hasn't been updated in a while.

Best for: Price-sensitive buyers who don't mind waiting for a good sale and are okay with standard quality. If your main goal is getting photos off your phone and into a physical book at the lowest cost, Snapfish delivers.

5. ReMeet - the done-for-you alternative

ReMeet takes a fundamentally different approach. Instead of giving you an editor, it gives you a design team. You share a link to your Google Photos album or cloud folder, and ReMeet's team - assisted by AI - does everything: photo selection, duplicate removal, cropping, layout, captions, and quality review.

The result looks more like a magazine or a coffee-table book than a typical photo book. Pages don't repeat. Layouts change. Full-bleed photos mix with white space. Chapter titles appear where they make sense. It reads like a story, not a grid of thumbnails.

Pricing: Digital book $29 (30 pages) to $79 (100 pages). Printing from $69. See our pricing comparison for how this stacks up.

Your effort: Zero. You share a link. You get a proof in 72 hours. You request edits by sending a page number and a note. That's the entire process.

The tradeoff: You don't control the design in real time. There's no drag-and-drop editor. If you enjoy spending an afternoon arranging photos and tweaking layouts, ReMeet isn't for you - it's for people who would never finish that project.

Best for: People with thousands of photos who want a beautiful book but don't have time (or patience) to design it themselves. Especially strong for year-in-review books, baby's first year books, and vacation photo books where photo overload is the main obstacle.

How to choose the right Mixbook alternative

The real question isn't "which service is better?" - it's "what do I actually need?"

If you want cheaper and don't mind basic design: Shutterfly or Snapfish during a sale.

If you want fast, small, and effortless: Chatbooks with a monthly subscription.

If you want higher quality and enjoy designing: Artifact Uprising.

If you want a professional result without doing any work: ReMeet.

Common questions when switching from Mixbook

Can I import my Mixbook project somewhere else?
No. Mixbook projects can't be exported to other services. You'd need to start fresh. If you've already selected photos, save them to a folder and upload them to the new service (or share the folder with ReMeet).

Is Mixbook's print quality the best?
It's good but not the best. Artifact Uprising has noticeably better paper and materials. ReMeet uses professional printing with premium paper options. Shutterfly and Chatbooks are a step below on print quality.

What if I have thousands of photos?
This is where Mixbook struggles the most. The editor assumes you've already culled your photos down to the final set. If you have 2,000+ photos from a year, you need to sort them first, which is hours of work before you even open the editor. ReMeet accepts albums of any size - AI selects the best shots and removes duplicates automatically.

What's the cheapest alternative to Mixbook?
By sticker price, Chatbooks ($10-15 softcover) or Snapfish during a sale. By total cost including your time, ReMeet at $29 for a digital book with zero hours of effort. For a detailed breakdown, see Cheapest Photo Book Services in 2026.

Mixbook is a good service. If you enjoy the design process and catch a sale, it's a solid choice. But if you're looking at alternatives, you're probably experiencing one of its real limitations: it takes too long, costs too much at full price, or still looks like a template after all that work.

Each alternative solves a different part of that problem. Shutterfly and Snapfish solve the price problem. Chatbooks solves the time problem for small books.
