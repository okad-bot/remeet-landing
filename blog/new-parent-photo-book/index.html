The new parent photo problem

Your baby rolls over and you take a photo. Then four more. Then a video. Then a burst of twelve trying to catch that smile. By the end of month one, you have 400 photos. By month twelve, somewhere around 3,000.

You know you should make a book. You tell yourself you'll do it on the weekend. The weekend comes and you're too tired, or the baby is teething, or there's laundry. You open a photo book app, stare at the grid of 3,000 thumbnails, and close it.

This isn't a failure of motivation. It's a math problem. Sorting 3,000 photos into a 30-page book means choosing about 100 and discarding 2,900. That takes hours. Hours that new parents do not have.

I built ReMeet's baby photo book for exactly this situation. You share the album. I do the sorting, the selecting, and the design. The book exists. The guilt goes away.

You don't need to organize anything before sharing your album. I sort by date and group photos into natural chapters. Here's what a typical first-year book covers:

Some parents include scanned handwritten notes, hospital wristbands, or height-and-weight cards. All of that works. If it's in your album or you mention it in the order notes, it goes in the book.

How many photos to send

All of them. Seriously. Don't spend time picking your best shots first. That's my job.

Share your entire Google Photos album or iCloud link. I go through every photo, remove the blurry ones, skip the duplicates, and pick the best version of each moment. If you took twelve burst shots of one smile, I find the sharpest one.

For a 30-page book, I typically select 80 to 120 photos out of whatever you send. For a 100-page book, 250 to 400. The more photos you share, the better the selection. You can send up to 5,000.

What the finished book looks like

Every book is designed page by page. No templates. No auto-fill grids. I pair photos that belong together, leave white space where the eye needs it, and add short captions like "June, learning to sit" or "First trip to the park."

The book reads like a story. It starts with the earliest photos and moves forward through the year. Big milestones get full-page spreads. Quiet moments get their space too. The sleeping-in-the-car-seat photo next to the first-bath photo next to the blurry-but-precious video-call-with-grandma screenshot. That's the real first year.

You get the designed book as a PDF proof. Flip through it, tell me what to change in plain words ("swap the bath photo on page 7," "add more photos from September"). Two rounds of revisions are included. Nothing prints until you approve.

Frequently asked questions

My photos are mostly on my phone. Is that OK?

Yes. Most parents send photos straight from their phone. Share an iCloud link or Google Photos album and I work from that. No need to download or upload individual files. Screen recordings, screenshots, and blurry shots get filtered out automatically.

Can I include ultrasound photos?

Absolutely. Ultrasound images, hospital wristbands, weight and height cards, even a photo of the positive pregnancy test. Anything that's part of the story can go in. Just include them in your album or mention them in the notes when you order.

What about photos from family members?

You can combine photos from multiple people. Ask grandparents and relatives to add their photos to a shared Google Photos album, or send you a separate link. I merge everything and pick the best shots across all sources.
