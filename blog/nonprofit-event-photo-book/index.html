Why a photo book beats a slideshow for donors

After a gala, a fundraiser, or a volunteer day, most nonprofits send donors a thank-you email with a few photos attached. Some put together a slideshow. Both get opened once and forgotten.

A printed nonprofit event photo book is different. It sits on a shelf. It stays on a coffee table in a board member's office. Major donors keep it next to the annual report. It's a physical reminder that their money did something real.

Slideshows disappear into inboxes. PDFs live in download folders nobody opens twice. A book lasts. And when it's time to ask for next year's gift, that book is still sitting there, doing your job for you.

That's what ReMeet helps you build. You share the photos. I handle the design, the layout, and the printing.

What events to include

The strongest nonprofit event photo books cover a full season or fiscal year. One event alone can fill a 30-page book, but combining several events tells a bigger story. Think about including:

  • Annual gala or fundraising dinner
  • Volunteer days and community service events
  • Awareness walks, runs, or outdoor activities
  • Board retreats and planning sessions
  • Program activities and beneficiary stories
  • Holiday parties and donor appreciation events
  • Ground-breaking ceremonies or facility openings

The more variety, the better the book looks. A mix of formal events and candid behind-the-scenes moments gives donors a full picture of where their support goes.

Getting photos from staff and volunteers

This is usually the hardest part. Photos live on ten different phones across your team. Here's what works:

  1. Create a shared Google Drive folder. Name it something clear like "2026 Event Photos." Share the link with staff, volunteers, and board members. Anyone with the link can upload.
  2. Send the link after every event. A quick email or Slack message right after the event gets the best results. People forget after a week.
  3. Set a final deadline. Two weeks after your last event of the year. Send one reminder.
  4. Share the folder link with me. I pull everything from the shared folder, sort by date, remove duplicates and blurry shots, and pick the strongest images for the layout.

A shared Google Photos album works just as well. So do iCloud links, Dropbox, or any cloud folder. The format doesn't matter. I work with whatever you have.
