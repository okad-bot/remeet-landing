What goes in a season book

A season book is a record of your group's year. Not a slideshow. Not a folder of photos nobody opens. A designed, printed book that people keep on a shelf and actually look at.

Here's what usually goes in one:

  • Group and team photos from the start and end of the season
  • Events and games in chronological order
  • Volunteers, coaches, and leadership featured on dedicated pages
  • Milestones like championship wins, fundraising goals met, or project completions
  • Thank-you spreads for sponsors, donors, or parent volunteers
  • A letter or message from the director, coach, or board
  • Rosters and stats if it's a sports team

The result is something between a yearbook and an annual report, but designed like a coffee table book. No clip art, no newsletter layouts, no stock borders. Clean pages with real photos, good spacing, and a consistent style throughout.

How it works with ReMeet

The process is the same whether you have 100 photos or 2,000.

  1. Create a shared album. Use Google Photos or iCloud. Add your photographer, coaches, parents, or anyone with photos. Everyone dumps their shots into one place. Here's how to set up a Google Photos album.
  2. Share the link with me. Pick your page count (30 or 100 pages) and pay the design fee. I'll sort the photos by date, remove duplicates and blurry frames, and pick the strongest shots.
  3. Get a designed proof in 72 hours. I lay out every page by hand. No templates. If you have sponsor logos, rosters, or a letter to include, send those along and I'll add them.
  4. Request changes. Two rounds of revisions included. Tell me what to adjust in plain words.
  5. Order copies. Once you approve, order as many printed copies as you need at $69 each. Each member gets a book. Shipping to the US and Canada is included.

The whole process takes about a week from the moment you share the album to the moment copies ship. If you need it faster, I can prioritize. If you have a large group contributing photos, give the album a few days to fill up before sharing it with me.

Making it a yearly tradition

The best season books are the ones that happen every year. The first one is a nice surprise. By year three, it's a tradition people look forward to. By year five, you have a shelf of matching volumes that tell the story of your group.

To make it easy to repeat:

  • Assign one person to manage the shared album each season
  • Remind contributors to add photos within a week of each event
  • Order the book within a month of the season ending, while memories are fresh
  • Keep the same page count and format each year so the books match on a shelf
