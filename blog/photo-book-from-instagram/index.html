How to Turn Your Instagram Into a Photo Book

November 2026 · 9 min read
Written by Olga Miller, founder of ReMeet

Your Instagram feed is probably the closest thing you have to a visual diary. Years of trips, birthdays, milestones, and random Tuesday mornings — all scrollable, all forgettable. Turning those photos into a physical book gives them a permanence that a feed never will.

But there's a catch most people don't know about: Instagram compresses every photo you upload. That matters when you try to print. And the different services handle this problem differently.

Here are five ways to turn your Instagram into a photo book, from fully automatic to fully custom — with honest trade-offs for each.

Before you start: the Instagram quality problem

Every photo you upload to Instagram gets compressed. The platform caps images at roughly 1080 pixels wide. Your phone's camera shoots at 4000-5000 pixels wide. That's a 75%+ reduction in detail.

For scrolling on a phone screen, 1080 pixels looks sharp. For printing a full-page photo in a book, it doesn't. A full-bleed page in a large (11x8.5 inch) photo book needs at least 2400 pixels wide for crisp results. Instagram's compressed version will look soft, sometimes noticeably blurry.

The golden rule

Whenever possible, use the original photos from your camera roll or a cloud backup like Google Photos, not the compressed versions from Instagram. Your phone kept the full-resolution file. Use that one.

If the originals are gone — you switched phones, didn't back up, or the photos only exist on Instagram — you can still make a good book. Just keep the photos at smaller sizes on the page rather than full-bleed, and the compression will be less visible. This is one of the common photo book mistakes: using low-resolution images at full-page size.

1. Chatbooks — direct Instagram import

Chatbooks is the fastest path from Instagram to a physical book. You connect your IG account, select a date range or photo set, and Chatbooks pulls the images directly. No downloading, no uploading. The book arrives in a few days.

How it works: Connect Instagram → select photos → auto-layout fills a 6x6 inch softcover or 8x8 hardcover. One photo per page, caption below. Subscription option: a new book ships automatically every month or quarter with your latest posts.

Pricing: Softcover starts around $10-15. Hardcover $30-40 for 60 pages.

Best for: Quick, casual documentation. People who want a small printed copy of their feed with zero effort. The monthly subscription model is genuinely clever for people who post regularly.

The catch: Uses Instagram's compressed photos, so print quality is limited. Design is basic — one photo per page, no multi-photo layouts, no chapter structure, no curation. The book feels like a printed feed, not a designed photo book. If you want to see how it stacks up against other budget services, the price is competitive but the format is limited.

2. MySocialBook — Instagram/Facebook scraper

MySocialBook takes a different approach: it scrapes your entire Instagram or Facebook profile and auto-generates a book with photos, captions, likes, and comments. The result looks like a printed version of your social media timeline.

How it works: Authorize access → MySocialBook imports your posts, stories, and metadata → auto-layout generates a book with every post in chronological order. You can edit the layout before ordering.

Pricing: Starts around $15 for softcover. Hardcover with 50+ pages runs $30-50.

Best for: People who want their social media history archived as a physical object — including comments and engagement data. It's a time capsule of your online life.

The catch: Same compression problem as Chatbooks: it pulls Instagram's downsized images. The auto-layout includes everything you posted, including throwaway stories and posts you'd rather forget. Design is functional, not beautiful. And because it includes comments and likes, it reads more like a social media scrapbook than a photo book.

3. DIY with Mixbook or Shutterfly

If you want full control over design, you can download your Instagram photos and upload them to a traditional photo book editor like Mixbook or Shutterfly.

How it works:

  1. Download your Instagram data (Settings → Your Activity → Download Your Information)
  2. Wait for the export email (usually 1-24 hours)
  3. Extract the photos from the downloaded zip file
  4. Upload them to Mixbook, Shutterfly, or another editor
  5. Design every page yourself — choose layouts, place photos, add text

Pro tip: Instead of using the Instagram export, use the original photos from your camera roll or Google Photos. They'll be much higher resolution. You can cross-reference dates with your IG feed to find the same shots. It's more work upfront, but the print quality is dramatically better.

Pricing: Mixbook 80-page hardcover runs $80-130. Shutterfly is $50-90. Both run frequent 40-50% sales.

Best for: People who enjoy the design process and want creative control. If you like spending a few hours choosing layouts and tweaking placements, Mixbook's editor is genuinely good. See our layout tips for making the most of a DIY editor.

The catch: It's a multi-step process. Downloading, extracting, uploading, then designing 60+ pages takes most people 4-6 hours total. And like any DIY project, many people start and never finish. The unfinished project sits in your cart until the session expires.

4. Google Photos → photo book

If you back up your photos to Google Photos (most Android phones do this by default; iPhones can too), Google's built-in photo book maker is an underrated option. The key advantage: Google Photos stores your original-quality images, not Instagram's compressed versions.

How it works: Open Google Photos → Print Store → Photo Book → select an album or let Google suggest photos. Google's AI auto-selects and arranges photos. You can rearrange, add, or remove.

Pricing: 7x7 inch softcover starts at $15 for 20 pages. 9x9 hardcover around $25 for 20 pages, plus $0.50-$1 per additional page.

Best for: People who already use Google Photos and want something simple without installing another app. The print quality is decent because it uses full-resolution images. For a broader look at AI-powered options, see our best AI photo book makers comparison.

The catch: Limited design options — a handful of templates, minimal text control, no chapter structure. The AI's photo selection is hit-or-miss: it often picks random screenshots or near-duplicates. You'll need to manually curate, which brings you back to the effort problem. Book sizes are also limited compared to dedicated services.

How to download your Instagram photos

If you do need to use Instagram as your source — maybe the originals are gone from your phone — here's how to get your photos out.

Method 1: Instagram's data download

  1. Open Instagram → Settings and Privacy → Your Activity → Download Your Information
  2. Select "Some of your information" → check "Posts" (and "Stories" if you want those)
  3. Choose format: HTML is easier to browse, JSON is for developers
  4. Choose quality: "High" (this is still the compressed version, but the best Instagram can offer)
  5. Submit the request. Instagram emails a download link within a few hours to a few days.
  6. Download and extract the zip. Your photos are in the media/posts/ folder.

Method 2: Save from camera roll instead

If you have "Save Original Photos" turned on in Instagram's settings (or if your phone backs up to Google Photos automatically), the full-resolution versions of every photo you posted to Instagram already exist on your device or in the cloud. Search by date in Google Photos to find the same shots. This gives you 4-5x the resolution of the Instagram export.

Which approach is right for you?

If you want it fast and cheap and don't mind basic design: Chatbooks. Connect IG, pick photos, done.

If you want a social media scrapbook with comments and likes: MySocialBook. It's a niche product but does this one thing well.

If you want full creative control: Download your photos and use Mixbook or Shutterfly. Budget 3-6 hours.

If you already back up to Google Photos: Start with Google's built-in maker for something simple, or share the album with ReMeet for something designed.

The real issue isn't Instagram — it's starting

Most people who think about turning their Instagram into a photo book never actually do it. They research services, compare prices, maybe download their data — and then the project stalls. The sorting feels overwhelming. The editor feels tedious. The tab stays open for a week, then gets closed.

The best approach is the one you'll actually finish. If that's a $12 Chatbooks softcover of your last 60 posts, that's a real book on a real shelf. If it's a $29 ReMeet book made from your Google Photos album, that's a book you'll flip through for years.
