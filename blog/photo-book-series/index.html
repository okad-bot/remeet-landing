One Book or a Series? How to Split a Year into Books

Updated Nov 2026 · by Olga, designer at ReMeet, 10 years in editorial design

If you have more photos than fit in one book, split them into a series. A 30-page book covers about 3-4 months or one event. A 100-page book covers a full year. With ReMeet, you share your album link, I sort the photos by date and suggest how to split them. Each book starts at $29.

When one book is enough

One book works when the photos share a single story. A wedding, a vacation, a baby's first three months, a school year. If the beginning and the end feel obvious, one book is probably right.

A good rule: if you can describe the book in one sentence ("our trip to Portugal" or "Mia's first year"), it fits in one volume. If the description gets long ("everything from 2022 to 2025, plus the renovation and the dog"), you probably need a series.

How to split a series

There are three natural ways to divide a photo collection. Pick the one that matches how you think about the photos.

By time period

The most common split. One book per year, one per quarter, or one per season. This works well for family yearbooks and childhood series. A family with two kids might order one book every December covering January through December. After five years, you have five matching volumes on a shelf.

By event or trip

Some photos don't belong to a time period. They belong to a place or an experience. A honeymoon book, a renovation book, a "grandpa's farm" book. Each one stands on its own but they look good together as a set.

By person

Less common but sometimes exactly right. A baby book for each child. A birthday book for a 40th and a separate one for a 50th. Individual books for each grandparent, each with the photos that matter most to them.

Examples that work

A year in four quarterly books. January through March in one slim 30-page volume. April through June in the next. Each book costs $29 for the design. By the end of the year you have four books that together tell the whole story, but each one is easy to flip through on its own.

A childhood in annual volumes. One book per year from birth to age five. The first covers those overwhelming newborn months. The second captures the first steps and words. By the time they start school, you have five books, each around 30-50 pages. The whole set costs less than one professional portrait session.

A trip series. One couple I worked with had photos from twelve trips over six years. We made one book per trip, with a consistent cover style so they line up on a shelf like a travel journal collection.

Your year in photos is a good starting point if you want to try a single volume first and decide later whether to continue as a series.

Keeping a series consistent

A series looks best when every volume shares the same design language. That means the same fonts, the same amount of white space, the same caption style. When you order a second book from me, just say "match the first one" and I carry over all the design decisions.

Some things that tie a series together:

  • Consistent cover layout (same position for the title, same font)
  • Matching spine text (Vol. 1, Vol. 2, or year numbers)
  • Same paper and binding (hardcover or layflat across all volumes)
  • A recurring element, like a handwritten note on the last page of each book

You don't need to plan the whole series before starting. Many people order one book, like it, and come back for the next one. I keep your design on file so matching is easy even a year later.
