Why a photo book works as a retirement gift

Gift cards get spent. Plaques collect dust. A photo book gets opened at the party, shown to family, and kept on a shelf. It says "we remember all of this" in a way a card can't.

I've designed retirement books for teachers, executives, nurses, and coaches. The photos don't have to be professional. Office selfies, team dinners, conference candids, and blurry holiday party shots all work. What matters is the collection, not the quality of individual images.

How to organize it

  1. Pick a point person. One colleague coordinates. They create a shared album, set a deadline, and send reminders.
  2. Collect photos. Create a shared Google Photos album or Google Drive folder. Send the link to the team and to the retiree's close colleagues from previous roles if possible. Ask for: team photos, office moments, events, achievements, and anything that captures who this person was at work.
  3. Collect messages (optional). Send a Google Form with one question: "What would you like to say to [name]?" Short notes from 10-15 people fill a few dedicated pages in the book.
  4. Share everything with me. Album link plus any messages. I do the rest.

Tips for a better book

  • Go beyond the office. Photos from company trips, charity events, or team outings add variety. If the retiree played on the company softball team, those photos belong in the book.
  • Include early career photos. Reach out to colleagues from the person's first years at the company. Old photos are often the most memorable.
  • Keep messages short. One to three sentences per person is better than a long letter. "You taught me more about patience than anyone. Enjoy the garden." That's enough.
  • Don't tell the retiree. This works best as a surprise at the retirement party. Have the point person collect everything quietly.

Frequently asked questions

How do we collect photos from colleagues?

Create a shared Google Photos album or Google Drive folder and send the link to the team. Ask people to upload any photos they have of the retiree: team events, office moments, conferences, holiday parties. Set a deadline two weeks before the retirement date.

How much does a retirement book cost?

Design is $29 for up to 30 pages or $79 for up to 100 pages. Each printed copy is $69 (hardcover) or from $129 (layflat). A 30-page book with 3 copies comes to $29 + 3 x $69 = $236. Split that across the team and it's very affordable.

Can we include written messages from the team?

Yes. Collect short notes from colleagues (a Google Form works well) and send them to me. I'll place them next to relevant photos or on dedicated message pages. Handwritten notes can also be scanned and included.

What if we don't have many photos?

Even 30-50 photos can make a meaningful 30-page book. Fill gaps with written messages, career milestones listed on a timeline page, or scanned memorabilia like awards and event programs.

How fast can you turn it around?

First draft in 72 hours from when you share the photos. Revisions take 24 hours each. If you need a printed copy, add 1-2 weeks for printing and shipping. Plan to share photos at least 3 weeks before the retirement party.
