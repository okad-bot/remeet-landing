Shutterfly vs Mixbook in 2026: Which Is Actually Better?

November 2026 · 9 min read

Shutterfly and Mixbook are the two biggest names in DIY photo books. Both let you upload photos, choose a template, and build a book page by page. Both run constant sales. Both ship in about a week.

So which one should you pick?

We compared them across six dimensions that actually matter when you're deciding where to spend your time and money: editor experience, design quality, pricing, print quality, turnaround, and ease of use. Here's what we found.

Neither service is objectively "better." They serve different priorities. Let's break each dimension down.

1. Editor experience

Shutterfly's editor gets the job done, but it feels like software from 2018. The drag-and-drop works, but it's not always responsive. Moving photos between pages requires multiple clicks. The auto-fill feature places photos randomly, so you'll spend time rearranging. Undo sometimes doesn't work the way you expect.

Mixbook's editor is noticeably more modern. Drag-and-drop is smoother, layout swapping is faster, and the whole interface feels more intuitive. Mixbook recently added an AI Story Mode that auto-generates a starting layout, which saves significant time even if you still want to customize.

Winner: Mixbook. The editor is the single biggest difference between these two services. If you're going to spend 2-4 hours designing a book, the tool you use matters.

2. Design quality

Both services offer hundreds of templates. The difference is how they look.

Shutterfly templates tend toward the safe and familiar: bordered layouts, solid backgrounds, decorative elements. They're fine for a quick project, but the results can look generic. If you've seen one Shutterfly baby book, you've seen most of them.

Mixbook templates are designed with more editorial awareness. Cleaner typography, better use of white space, more variety in page compositions. The best Mixbook templates produce books that look closer to what a designer might create. For tips on what makes a layout work, see our photo book layout tips.

Winner: Mixbook. This is one of the main reasons Tom's Guide and other reviewers consistently rate Mixbook as the "best overall" photo book service. The templates simply look better.

3. Pricing

This is where Shutterfly fights back. For a detailed pricing breakdown of all major services, see our cheapest photo book services comparison.

Shutterfly pricing:

  • Softcover books start around $10
  • 80-page hardcover: $50-90 at list price
  • Sales run almost constantly: 40-50% off is the norm
  • Effective price after a typical sale: $25-50 for an 80-page hardcover

Mixbook pricing:

  • Softcover books start around $30
  • 80-page hardcover: $80-130 at list price
  • Sales are frequent but slightly less deep than Shutterfly's
  • Effective price after a typical sale: $40-65 for an 80-page hardcover

Winner: Shutterfly. If budget is your primary concern, Shutterfly is consistently cheaper, especially during sales. The gap narrows when both services run promotions, but Shutterfly still comes out ahead on price.

Keep in mind that both services require you to design the book yourself, which takes 2-5 hours. If you factor in your time, the price gap looks different. We break this down in our DIY vs professional photo book comparison.

4. Print quality

Both services print in the US and produce decent hardcover books. The differences are subtle but real.

Shutterfly print quality is good for the price. Colors are reasonably accurate, pages are sturdy, and the binding holds up. However, quality can vary between orders. Some customers report inconsistent color reproduction, especially with darker images. Shutterfly uses standard paper by default.

Mixbook print quality is a step up. Colors are more consistent, pages feel slightly thicker, and the overall construction is more polished. Mixbook also offers more paper type options, including premium matte and semi-gloss. Their layflat binding option is excellent for panoramic photos and full-spread layouts.

Winner: Mixbook. Not by a huge margin, but reviewers and customers consistently rate Mixbook's print quality slightly higher. If you're making a special occasion book (wedding, milestone birthday), the quality difference is worth paying for.

5. Turnaround time

Both services ship from US facilities with similar timelines:

  • Shutterfly: 5-9 business days standard shipping. Rush options available.
  • Mixbook: 5-8 business days standard shipping. Express available at extra cost.

Neither is significantly faster than the other. Both can deliver in about a week for standard orders, or 3-4 days if you pay for rush shipping.

Winner: Tie. There's no meaningful difference in shipping speed.

Note that turnaround only counts from when you place the order. The real bottleneck is how long it takes you to design the book. Most people take days, weeks, or months to finish. For a realistic time breakdown, see our guide to the easiest photo book services.

6. Ease of use

This is where the comparison gets interesting, because "ease of use" means different things.

Shutterfly has been around since 1999. The sheer breadth of products (books, cards, mugs, blankets, wall art) means the interface tries to do everything. Navigation can feel cluttered. The photo book editor itself is straightforward once you find it, but the surrounding experience has a lot of noise.

Mixbook is more focused. It does books, cards, and calendars. The interface is cleaner, the path from "upload photos" to "start designing" is shorter, and the editor is more forgiving of mistakes. The AI Story Mode feature can generate a first draft of your book automatically, which significantly lowers the barrier to getting started.

Winner: Mixbook. Fewer clicks to get started, a smoother editor, and AI assistance make it the easier option for first-timers.

What Shutterfly does better

Despite losing on several dimensions, Shutterfly has real advantages:

  • Price. Consistently cheaper, with deeper and more frequent sales.
  • Product variety. If you want to turn photos into mugs, blankets, ornaments, or wall art alongside your book, Shutterfly is a one-stop shop.
  • Brand recognition. Shutterfly is the household name. Gift recipients know it. If you're ordering for someone else and they want to reorder, they'll know where to go.
  • Walgreens pickup. Shutterfly offers same-day pickup at Walgreens for certain products. Mixbook doesn't have a retail partner.

What Mixbook does better

  • Editor. More modern, more intuitive, less frustrating.
  • Templates. Better-designed starting points that require less customization to look good.
  • Print quality. More consistent, with better paper options.
  • AI assistance. Story Mode gives you a head start instead of a blank canvas.
  • Focus. Fewer products means a cleaner experience for people who just want a photo book.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Shutterfly if:

  • Budget is your top priority
  • You want to order other photo products (cards, gifts) alongside your book
  • You're comfortable with a more basic editor
  • You need same-day pickup at a retail store

Choose Mixbook if:

  • Design quality matters more than price
  • You want a smoother, more modern editing experience
  • You're making a special occasion book (wedding, anniversary, milestone)
  • You want AI help getting started

The third option: what if you don't want to design at all?

Here's the thing both Shutterfly and Mixbook have in common: they expect you to do all the work. You sort your photos. You choose layouts. You place each image. You crop. You write captions. You review every page.

That's 2-5 hours of design work, regardless of which service you pick. And for a lot of people, that's the reason they never finish the book.

Done-for-you services take a fundamentally different approach. You share a link to your photos. A team handles everything: sorting, duplicate removal, cropping, layout, captions, and quality review. You get a designed proof to review and request changes.
