What makes a coffee table book different

A coffee table book is not a photo album with more pages. The design is different because the purpose is different. It sits in your living room. People pick it up. Every spread needs to hold attention on its own.

FeatureRegular photo bookCoffee table book
LayoutGrid of 3-6 photos per page1-2 photos per spread, full bleed
White spaceMinimal, photos fill every cornerGenerous, photos breathe
TextCaptions under each photoMinimal or none
Photo selectionInclude most of your favoritesOnly the strongest shots
PacingChronologicalMood and visual rhythm
Best printHardcoverLayflat (pages open flat)

The biggest difference is restraint. A good coffee table book says less, and each page hits harder because of it.

Best source photos for coffee table books

Coffee table books look best when the source material is strong. Here is what works well:

  • Wedding photos. The ceremony, the portraits, the candids, the venue details. A photographer gives you 500 to 2,000 images. I pick the 60 to 100 that tell the story best.
  • Travel photography. Landscapes, street scenes, food, architecture. The mix of wide shots and close details makes for interesting spreads.
  • Family portrait sessions. Lifestyle shoots with natural light, outdoor settings, kids being kids. Even 50 great photos from one session can fill a beautiful 30-page book.
  • Real estate and architecture. Interior and exterior shots of a home, a renovation project, or a property portfolio.
  • Event photography. Galas, conferences, fundraisers. Organizations often want a book to share with donors and board members.

Phone photos can work too, especially newer iPhones and high-end Android cameras. The key is good light and sharp focus. If the photo looks good on a large screen, it will print well.

How I design a coffee table book

The process is the same as any ReMeet book, but the design approach is different.

  1. You share the photos. Send a Google Drive, Dropbox, or iCloud link. High-res JPEGs or TIFFs are ideal. I accept RAW files too.
  2. I select and sequence. I go through every photo and pick the ones with the strongest composition, light, and emotion. Then I arrange them in an order that flows, not just chronologically but visually. A wide landscape followed by a tight portrait. A quiet moment after something energetic.
  3. I design every page. Full-bleed spreads where the photo earns it. White space where the eye needs rest. Minimal text if you want it. No templates, no grids, no auto-layout.
  4. You review and request changes. I send a proof in 72 hours. You tell me what to adjust in plain words. Two rounds of edits included.
  5. You approve and print. Download the PDF or order a printed copy. For coffee table books, I recommend the premium layflat, where pages open completely flat.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send RAW files for my coffee table book?

Yes. Share them through Google Drive, Dropbox, or any cloud link. I work with JPEG, TIFF, and RAW files. If you have both RAW and edited JPEGs, send the edited versions. They already have the color and exposure your photographer intended.

What size is the coffee table book?

The standard ReMeet book is 21x21 cm (about 8.3x8.3 inches) square. This is a comfortable coffee table size that works for both portrait and landscape photos through careful cropping and full-bleed layouts.

Can I add captions to a coffee table book?

Yes. Minimal captions work well for coffee table books. Locations, dates, or short notes. Tell me what you want on each page, or send a list and I place them. Many coffee table books work best with little or no text, letting the photos speak.

Should I choose hardcover or layflat?

Layflat is the better choice for coffee table books. Pages open completely flat, so a photo can run across both pages without losing anything in the spine. Hardcover is lighter and more affordable but has a small gutter in the center of each spread.

How many photos do I need?

For a 30-page book, 40 to 80 photos is a good range. For 100 pages, 150 to 300 works well. More is fine. I select the strongest shots and give each one the space it needs. A coffee table book is about quality, not quantity.
