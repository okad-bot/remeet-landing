Creative baby book ideas

Monthly milestones

The classic approach, and for good reason. One spread per month with the monthly blanket photo, the biggest developments, and a note about what changed. Watching a newborn become a walking, babbling almost-toddler across twelve spreads is breathtaking.

The "people who love you" chapter

Dedicate a section to every person who held your baby in the first year. Grandparents, aunts, uncles, friends, neighbors. A page for each, with their name, their relationship, and the moment they first met. Your child will look at these pages for decades.

The book of firsts

First bath. First solid food. First laugh. First steps. First haircut. First time at the beach. Organize the whole book around firsts, with each one getting its own spread. It turns the book into a record of discovery — everything your baby experienced for the very first time.

Everyday moments

Not every photo needs to be a milestone. The 6am bedhead, the banana-smeared high chair, the way they slept in the car seat. These ordinary moments are the ones you forget fastest, and the ones that hit hardest when you look back five years from now.

What to include in your baby photo book

  • Birth story — The hospital, the first moment you held your baby, the exhaustion and the joy. Even if the photos are blurry, they matter.
  • Coming home — The car seat that looked enormous, the nursery reveal, the first night in their own room (or yours)
  • Monthly growth — Same blanket, same spot, twelve months of change. If you missed some months, that's fine — include what you have
  • Feeding moments — Breastfeeding, bottles, the first spoonful of rice cereal, the sweet potato disaster
  • Sleep — Babies sleep in the most absurd positions. Those photos are pure gold ten years from now
  • Bath time — The tiny tub, the first real bath, the rubber ducks, the splashing
  • Family visits — Grandparents meeting the baby, sibling reactions, friends holding a newborn for the first time
  • Holidays — First Halloween costume, first Thanksgiving, first Christmas morning
  • Outings — First trip to the park, the zoo, the grocery store, the coffee shop with a stroller
  • Personality emerging — The facial expressions, the favorite toy, the thing that always made them laugh
  • Parents in the frame — You're in this story too. Include the selfies, the partner-shot candids, the tired smiles that show the reality of new parenthood

Pro tips for better baby photos

Use natural light. Sit near a window. Morning light is soft and warm and makes everything — especially baby skin — look beautiful. Turn off the flash.

Get down to their level. The best baby photos are taken from the baby's perspective, not standing above them. Lie on the floor. Kneel next to the changing table. Meet their eyes.

Capture the context. Don't just photograph the baby. Photograph the room, the mess, the half-eaten lunch on the counter, the sibling watching from the doorway. These details tell the real story.

Take photos of the mundane. The photos that move you ten years from now aren't the posed ones. They're the 3am feeding, the laundry pile with tiny socks on top, the view from the rocking chair. Photograph what Tuesday looks like.

Include yourself. You're sleep-deprived and haven't showered. That's the truth of the first year, and it's beautiful. Take the selfie. Hand the phone to your partner. Set up the timer. Future you will be grateful.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best age to start a baby photo book?

Right now. Seriously. The best time to start is whenever you're thinking about it. Many parents start during pregnancy or in the first weeks after birth, but there's no wrong time. If your baby is already six months old, you still have hundreds of photos worth organizing. If your baby is two, you can still make a gorgeous first-year book from the photos already on your phone.

How do I organize baby photos by month?

You don't have to. Send us all your photos and we'll sort them chronologically using the date metadata on each file. We'll create natural chapter breaks at each month and add milestone markers. If you have specific monthly photos (like blanket milestone shots), just let us know and we'll feature those as chapter openers.

Can I include ultrasound photos in the baby book?

Yes, and many parents do. Ultrasound photos make a beautiful opening chapter — the story before the story. If your ultrasounds are printed on thermal paper, photograph them with your phone now (they fade over time). We'll clean up the image quality and integrate them into the book's design alongside your other photos.

What if my baby is already a toddler — is it too late?

Not at all. Most parents who make baby photo books do it after the first year, not during it. You're too busy and too sleep-deprived in those early months to organize photos. The fact that your baby is a toddler means you have the full first year documented and the perspective to know which moments mattered most. That actually makes for a better book.

How many photos do I need for a baby photo book?

We recommend sharing at least 100-200 photos, but more is better — we'll curate the best ones. Most parents have thousands from the first year. Don't worry about duplicates, blurry shots, or screenshots mixed in. Send us everything and our designers will find the gems. A typical baby book uses 150-300 photos across 80-120 pages.

Can I add handwritten notes or letters to my baby?

Yes. Write your note on paper, photograph it, and send it to us — we'll include the handwritten image in the book. Or type out your message and we'll set it in beautiful typography on a dedicated page. Many parents include a letter at the beginning or end of the book, or short notes at each monthly milestone. These personal touches are often the most treasured pages.
