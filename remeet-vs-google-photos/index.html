Choose Google Photos Books if:

  • You want the simplest, fastest path from photos to a printed book
  • Your photos are already organized in Google Photos
  • You want a basic keepsake at the lowest possible price
  • You're comfortable with AI picking which photos to include
  • You don't need chapters, captions, or editorial layout

Choose Remeet if:

  • You want a book that tells a story, not just displays photos
  • You want human curation — someone who understands which moments matter
  • You care about print quality, paper weight, and binding
  • You want a coffee-table book, not a basic photo album
  • You've seen what Google Photos auto-generates and want something more intentional

The honest take

Google Photos Books is the easiest way to get a printed photo book. If your photos are in Google Photos, the app will even suggest books for you — pick one, tweak a few photos, and order. At $15-30, it's practically an impulse purchase. For a quick keepsake or a casual gift, it's hard to argue with that convenience.

The limitation is design. Google Photos books are simple grids of photos with no narrative structure, no captions, and no editorial layout. The paper is standard, the binding is basic, and the overall feel is "photo printout in book form" rather than "designed book."

Remeet exists in a different category. You get human curation, editorial design with chapters and captions, and premium materials. It takes 72 hours instead of 5 minutes, and costs 7-10x more. The question is straightforward: do you want a quick, affordable photo album, or a designed book that feels like it belongs on a shelf?
