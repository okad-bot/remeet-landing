Choose Snapfish if:

  • Budget is your top priority and you want the cheapest option
  • You want a quick, simple book and don't need premium materials
  • You like using coupons and catching deep-discount sales
  • You want auto-fill to do most of the work for free
  • You need multiple copies for family at the lowest cost

Choose Remeet if:

  • You want a premium product with archival-quality materials
  • You want professional curation and editorial design, not auto-fill
  • You have too many photos to sort through yourself
  • You want a coffee-table book, not just a photo album
  • You've tried budget photo books and want something that feels different in your hands

The honest take

Snapfish and Remeet aren't really competitors — they serve different needs at different price points. Snapfish is the best option if you want a decent photo book at the lowest possible price. With their frequent sales and coupons, you can get a hardcover book for under $20. That's genuinely impressive value.

The difference shows up in two places: materials and design. Snapfish pages are thinner, the printing is standard-grade, and the layouts are basic templates. Remeet uses archival paper, premium hardcovers, and each book gets a custom editorial layout with chapters and captions.

Think of it this way: Snapfish is a great photo album. Remeet is a coffee-table book. Both preserve your memories — one costs less, the other looks and feels like something you'd find in a bookstore.
